Extract from the investigation report RJ 2009:04 on a near-collision 
occurred on 09/06/2008 
Summary  
On Monday, 9 June 2008 a ballast plough was to be transported from Mölndals to Herrljunga 
via Gothenburg. The convoy travelled as a work train for transport, and the plough was hauled 
by its own engines. The ballast plough was not fitted with any train protection system, i.e. ATC 
(Automatic Train Control). The driver of the ballast plough was at the same time the supervisor 
for the convoy.  
When the ballast plough approached Bryngenäs station, there was a failure to notice the 
advance signal and its message that the next signal, the access signal, showed “stop”. It was 
only immediately before the ballast plough was about to pass the access signal that the stop 
signal was perceived and the brakes applied to the ballast plough. The ballast plough passed 
the access signal, drove up a shunt track and stopped on a subsequent shunt track.  
At the same time, a commuter train was travelling from Alingsås to Gothenburg in the opposite 
direction on the same track. The train had stopped at its access signal and driven in to the 
station when the signal changed to ”go”, in order to pass over to the other track via the shunt 
which the ballast plough was driving up. The driver of the train noticed that a vehicle was 
approaching him; since the train was travelling at a low speed it could be stopped immediately, 
which meant that a collision was avoided.  
The cause of the near-accident was that the work train for transport passed access signal 
Bryngenäs 22 which showed “stop”, without permission. The most likely reason for this was 
that the advance signal for the access signal was not noticed in time, and the access signal 
was not noticed until some time afterwards, when it had come into the supervisor’s field of 
vision.  
One reason why the work train was able to pass the signal is that the vehicle was not equipped 
with ATC.  
The investigation has been unable to establish with certainty why neither the advance signal 
nor the access signal were noticed in sufficiently good time. In the opinion of SHK the 
prerequisite conditions for this were good, i.e. the supervisor had good knowledge of the line 
and the visibility was good.

## Recommendations  
The Swedish Transport Agency is recommended to take steps as follows:  
 
introduce, as a matter of urgency, a requirement for the use of train protection systems in 
vehicles that run on infrastructure that is equipped with train protection systems (RJ 
2009:04 R1); 
 
improve the routines for the issue of permission and supervision, so that any lack of 
provisions that are essential for traffic safety can be detected (RJ 2009:04 R2).
