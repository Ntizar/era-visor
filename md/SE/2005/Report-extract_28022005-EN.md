Extract from the investigation report RJ 2007:2 on a collision with a 
buffer stop and subsequent derailment occurred on 28/02/2005 
Summary  
At 12:46 on Monday, 28 February 2005, the locomotive and the front wagons of freight train 
5525 left the rails after having passed a main signal showing ”stop” and subsequently driven 
through the buffer stop on the safety track which forms an extension of track 1 at Ledsgård 
station. 
Train 5525 consisted of an electric locomotive and 12 wagons loaded with chlorine. The 
wagons had been loaded in Bohus and after systems testing (safety check and brake test) 
driven to Sävenäs shunting yard with a diesel locomotive as train 6505. At Sävenäs the 
locomotive was changed and a new brake test performed before departure. 
During the preparations prior to departure at Bohus, there was a failure to check the position of 
the wagons’ s EMPTY/LOADED handle, which operates an empty/load changeover device and 
which affects braking capacity in relation to the load of the wagon. The braking capacity of the 
train was established according to a list of wagons with weight information which was based on 
the assumption that the wagons were loaded and that the handle was also in the correct 
position. 
The driver did not have access to the wagons list during the systems testing and the brake 
test. 
The EMPTY/LOADED handle, however, was in the position ”EMPTY”, which meant a very 
serious reduction of the train’s actual braking capacity. Despite the fact that both the driver of 
train 6505 on the way to Sävenäs shunting yard and the driver of train 5525 after the change of 
locomotive each carried out a brake test, they did not notice that the braking capacity of the 
train was inadequate. 
At Ledsgård station, train 5525 was supposed to be taken in and to stand on a siding track, 
track 1, in order to be passed by another train. At the station boundary the driver applied the 
brakes somewhat in order to reduce speed. When he subsequently saw that the points were 
set to track 1 and that intermediate signal 33 showed ”stop” he braked in order to stop. 
Immediately after that ATC (Automatic Train Control) kicked in with the service brakes. 
The shunting yard at Ledsgård slopes downwards in the train’s direction of travel. Next to the 
intended stopping place, there is a gradient of nine per thousand. This gradient was not 
encoded in the ATC instructions. 
If this coding had been in place, the ATC brakes would have been triggered earlier. 
The position of the EMPTY/LOADED handle and the gradient meant that the actual 
deceleration during braking was about half of what the driver expected and what was input into 
the ATC. 
Intermediate signal Lgd 33, where the train was supposed to stop, was passed at 43 km/h and 
the buffers at the track siding were hit at 39 km/h. The locomotive and almost three wagons 
passed the end of the track and stopped in the mud of the field which lay beyond the shunt 
track. The locomotive and the first four wagons sustained extensive damage. 
When the fire and rescue service arrived at the site of the accident it was established that 
there was no leakage of chlorine gas from the wagons. The damaged wagons were emptied

The direct reason why train 5525 could not stop before the stop signal at track 1 at Ledsgård 
was that the train’s actual braking capacity was seriously reduced due to the fact that all 
empty/load changeover devices were in the wrong position - EMPTY instead of LOADED. 
This was not detected when the train’s braking capacity was established at Bohus. 
At Ledsgård the operation of ATC was delayed when the speed was not reduced as expected, 
since there were no gradient codes in the ATC instructions. This would not have prevented the 
incident, but would have mitigated the consequences. The same applies if the buffers at the 
shunt track had been correctly designed. Both these circumstances have their background in 
inadequate planning routines. 
The shortcomings in the performance of switching, brake testing and establishment of brake 
capacity prior to the departure from Bohus may have their basis in inadequate training and 
routines, and the relatively rough-and-ready system for follow-up of duties which is applied. 
There is a lack of “barriers” which prevent a train from departing before a check has been 
carried out to establish that the position of the adjustment handle corresponds to the brake 
weights used in calculating the braking ratio. 
The system involving trial brakings and deceleration checks is not sufficient to provide an 
optimal detection of deviations from the theoretical braking ratio established for the train. 
## Recommendations  
The Swedish Accident Investigation Board (SHK) recommends that the Swedish Railways 
Board: 
 
take steps to ensure that the risk of individual faults in connection with the establishment of 
a train’s braking capacity is minimised, for example by the introduction of checklists or a 
separate entry that the position of the adjustment handle has been checked in ”duties of 
drivers” or similar (RJ 2007:2 R1); 
 
introduce requirements for follow-up monitoring of safety personnel, both shortly after basic 
training has been given or when the work duties have been changed, and regularly during 
their period of service (RJ 2007:2 R2); 
 
take steps to ensure that the planning and follow-up of work performed by operators is 
improved (RJ 2007:2 R3); 
 
take steps to ensure that infrastructure managers have procedures in their safety 
guidelines which mean that the risk of shortcomings in planning similar to those described 
in this report is minimised (RJ 2007:2 R4); 
 
consider whether there is a need to develop a set of comprehensive principles and 
standards for safety tracks with the focus on how safe braking of vehicles is to take place, 
with account taken of the risk of damage to the vehicle as well as the environment beyond 
the end of the track (RJ 2007:2 R5).
