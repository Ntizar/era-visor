#  THE INVESTIGATION AND ITS CONTEXT 
## 1. Decision to establish an investigation 
The Chairman of the State Commission on Railway Accident Investigation (hereinafter referred to as 
‘PKBWK’ or ‘the Commission’) Mr Tadeusz Ryś issued Decision no. PKBWK.590.3.2025 of 21 August 
2025 on establishing an investigation into the causes and circumstances of the accident at the Category D 
level crossing located in the Pierzchno - Gądki section at km 182.466 of railway line no. 272 Kluczbork - 
Poznań Główny. 
Taking this fact into account, and in accordance with the provisions of Article 28e(4) of the Rail Transport 
Act (consolidated text: Journal of Laws of 2024, item 697, as amended), hereinafter referred to as the ‘Rail 
Transport Act’, on 22 August 2025 the occurrence was reported to the European Union Railway Agency 
and was recorded in its database under reference number PL-10716. 
## 2. Motivation for the decision to establish an investigation 
On the basis of an analysis of the circumstances of the occurrence and taking into account its nature, the 
Chairman of the PKBWK decided that the Commission’s Investigation Team should initiate proceedings 
pursuant to Article 28e(3)(2) and (3) of the Rail Transport Act, given that the accident is part of a series of 
accidents affecting the system as a whole and has an impact on rail traffic safety.

#  DESCRIPTION OF THE OCCURRENCE 
## 1. The occurrence and background information 
### 1.1. Description of the type of occurrence 
While passenger train MOJ 17505, operated by POLREGIO S.A. on the Łódź Kaliska–Poznań Główny 
route, was travelling, at a category D level crossing (which is not fitted with an automatic level crossing 
system), the driver of a road vehicle (a BMW X3 passenger car) drove directly in front of train MOJ 
17505 (electric multiple unit EN57AL – 1222). The road vehicle entered the level crossing from the 
right side of the train. This resulted in the train colliding with the road vehicle. The front of the train 
struck the left-hand side of the car. As a result of the impact, the car was thrown onto the adjacent track 
no. 2 and came to a halt after approximately 143 metres at km 182.609. The driver of the car and three 
of his passengers died instantly. The rail vehicle was not derailed. The head of the train stopped at km 
183.280, i.e. 814 m from the centreline of the level crossing. 
### 1.2 The date, exact time and location of the occurrence 
The occurrence took place on 15 August 2025 at 1:52 p.m. in the Pierzchno–Gądki route section, on 
track no. 1, at a category D level crossing, at km 182.466 of line no. 272, Kluczbork – Poznań Główny, 
within the area managed by the infrastructure manager PKP PLK S.A., Maintenance-of-Way 
Department in Ostrów Wielkopolski, level crossing identification number 272 182 466 (yellow sticker 
on the St. Andrew’s Cross placed on the track side). 
The geographical coordinates of the level crossing are 5217’22”N, 1704’55”E. 
The Investigation Team determined the exact time of the occurrence by analysing the data from the

‘Train no. 17505 ready for departure’ at 1:50:55 p.m., the driver started the engine and the train departed

181.626, relating to the level crossing at km 182.466.

manager notified the POLREGIO S.A. establishment dispatcher in Poznań. The manager and the conductor 
then made their way towards the level crossing to check what had happened and, if necessary, to assist the 
victims. By the time they reached the level crossing at km 182.466, the emergency services were already 
at the scene.

## 3. Human factors 
### 3.1. Human and individual characteristics 
According to his family, the driver of the road vehicle was a good driver. He was mentally and physically 
fit. He had no health problems and was in good physical and mental condition. As far as they knew, he 
had no enemies or personal problems. The tests did not detect any of the most common groups of narcotic 
or psychotropic substances in the driver’s body. 
The driver of the passenger car, while approaching the level crossing, stopped the vehicle in front of the 
B-20 ‘stop’ sign, but then failed to exercise due care and, despite a passenger train approaching the level 
crossing, drove onto the level crossing directly in front of the oncoming train, despite the ‘Attention’ signal 
being sounded twice by the train driver. The driver of the car failed to respond to the signals given by the 
train driver and continued to drive onto the level crossing.

The Investigation Team concluded that the driver of the MOJ 17505 train had acted correctly immediately 
before the accident. Having noticed a car approaching from the right-hand side of the level crossing, he 
gave the Rp1 ‘Attention’ signal, and, upon realising that the driver of the road vehicle, having previously 
stopped at the B-20 ‘Stop’ sign, had set off again and driven onto the level crossing, he immediately 
initiated an emergency braking of the train while simultaneously sounding the Rp1 ‘Attention’ signal. 
Tests carried out on the train driver did not show the presence of alcohol or psychoactive substances in his 
organism. The train driver's work time was in compliance with the applicable standards. The train driver 
held the required documents and had received training required for operating traction vehicles, and had 
completed other training courses related to his job. 
The train manager did not see the accident itself, but he participated in the activities afterwards. He had 
the required documents and qualifications. A breathalyser test did not show any alcohol content in his 
breath. 
The Investigation Team considered the entry of the passenger car onto the Cat. d level crossing, directly 
in front of the incoming passenger train MOJ 17505, as the causal factor in the occurrence.  
The factors contributing to the accident were identified as the driver of the road vehicle failing to exercise 
due care when crossing the level crossing, the driver of the passenger car failing to check whether a railway 
vehicle was approaching the crossing, and the failure of the driver of the passenger car to respond to the 
Rp1 ‘Attention’ signal given by the driver of the MOJ 17505 train. 
### 3.2. Job factors 
The EN57AL - 1222 rail vehicle had been issued type approval T/2014/0254 for operation in the Polish

driver. 
The road vehicle – passenger car was in good working order at the time of the accident and had a technical 
inspection certificate valid until 30 January 2026.

### 3.4. Environmental factors 
The occurrence took place in good weather and visibility conditions, with no rain or fog. The occurrence 
took place during daylight hours (in the afternoon), under clear skies, with a temperature of around +33 
ºC. There were no other road users in the area of the level crossing at the time of the occurrence that could 
attract the attention of the driver of the road vehicle. No work was being carried out in the vicinity of the 
level crossing, and there were no advertisements that might have distracted the driver. The railway line 
and the road intersecting at the level crossing run through a tall mixed forest.

#  CONCLUSIONS 
## 1. A summary of the analysis and conclusions with regard to the causes of the 
occurrence 
The analysis carried out shows that the infrastructure manager is properly fulfilling its obligations regarding 
the maintenance of the infrastructure in the vicinity of the level crossing where the accident occurred.  
The Investigation Team found that the driver of the road vehicle, when approaching the level crossing, did 
not exercise particular care, despite the approaching train and the Rp1 ‘Attention’ signal given by the train 
driver, warning road users approaching the level crossing of an approaching train, and drove onto the level 
crossing directly in front of the MOJ 17505 train. 
The Investigation Team considered the causal factor to be: 
− 
entry of the passenger car onto the Cat. D level crossing directly in front of the oncoming passenger 
train MOJ 17505, which resulted in the train colliding with the car. 
The Investigation Team found the contributing factors to be: 
− 
failure by the driver of a BMW X3 passenger car to exercise due care, 
− 
the driver of the passenger car failing to check whether a train is approaching, 
− 
the car driver’s failure to respond to the Rp1 ‘Attention’ signal given by the driver of train MOJ 
17505. 
## 2. Measures taken since the occurrence 
The Chairman of the State Commission on Railway Accidents Investigation did not issue any 
recommendations requiring immediate action. 
## 3.1.3. Additional comments 
The Investigation Team identified the following other irregularities not related to the causal and 
contributing factors: 
1. Failure to correctly record certain driving characteristics for vehicle EN57AL – 1222, namely the 
pressure in the main pipe and the emission of the Rp1 ‘Attention’ signal. 
2. Poor visibility of the G1c sign from the direction of the car approaching the level crossing. 
3. Failure of the train driver to sound the required audible signals when passing certain W6b signs.  
4. The presence in the driver’s cab of an unauthorised person, who left the cab approximately 15 
minutes before the occurrence.
