#  DESCRIPTION OF THE OCCURRENCE 
## 1. Occurrence and background information 
### 1.1. Description of the type of occurrence 
Upon arrival at Wrocław Nadodrze station at 11.36, the driver of train ZUS 669226 began manoeuvring 
(pushing the train) from behind the Tm20 shunting signal towards track No. 209 of the SPRT depot, 
where the used sleepers were to be unloaded. Upon reaching the closed gate at turnout No. 203, he waited 
for an employee of the Wolf Security Agency, who opened it. Once the gate had been opened, the driver 
began to enter track No. 209, despite the fact that the conditions for proper observation of the route had 
not been met and despite the absence of the shunting foreman at the head of the train being pushed. The 
driver’s visibility was obstructed by the load on the flat wagons. Whilst entering the SPRT depot from 
beneath the gate onto track No. 209, at turnout No. 205, the train struck a security agency employee who, 
after opening the gate, was walking between the rails (on the track centerline) where the shunting 
manoeuvres were taking place. As a result of the collision, the security agency employee died at the scene. 
During the investigation, considering the impact of the occurrence on the Safety Management System 
and the presence of systemic factors, the Investigation Team decided to reclassify the occurrence as a 
serious accident.

#  ANALYSIS OF THE OCCURRENCE 
## 1.  Roles and responsibilities of the various parties involved in the occurrence 
### 1.1 Railway undertaking(s) or infrastructure manager(s) 
Infrastructure manager PKP PLK S.A. Railway Line Plant in Wrocław 
The railway infrastructure manager has established an SPRT depot, which is located at Wrocław Nadodrze 
station. The railway infrastructure manager has set out the rules governing the operation of the depot in 
the Wrocław Nadodrze Technical Regulations and the SPRT Organisational and Technical Regulations. 
In practice, the depot included a group of station tracks: 201, 201a, 201b, 201e, 203a, 205, 206, 207, 207a, 
209, 211, 213 and 215. Despite this, the Wrocław Nadodrze Technical Regulations did not include these 
tracks in its list of station tracks, whilst the SPRT Technical Regulations stated that the SPRT depot 
includes tracks 203a, 205, 206, 207 and 207a. Neither the Wrocław Nadodrze Technical Regulations nor 
the SPRT Technical Regulations set out detailed rules for carrying out shunting operations at the SPRT 
depot, including rules for notifying the need to open gates to allow a shunting movement to enter the depot; 
nor did they specify which staff members were responsible for opening and closing the entrance gates for 
railway vehicles.  
The Investigation Team identified this circumstance as a contributing factor to the serious accident.  
The Wrocław Nadodrze Technical Regulations database was updated on 10 October 2025 and 24 October 
2025, that is, only after the serious accident. Among other things, the list of tracks at Wrocław Nadodrze 
station forming part of the SPRT depot was updated. Furthermore, on 28 January 2026, a further 
amendment was made to the Wrocław Nadodrze Technical Regulations, namely to plot 51, in which 
persons responsible for operating all entry gates to the SPRT depot were designated and safe access routes 
were identified. This amendment implemented the recommendations of the State Labour Regional 
Inspection in Wrocław (hereinafter ‘PIP’), issued following PIP inspections conducted in October and 
November 2025, and in January and February 2026.  
Based on this, the investigation team decided not to issue any recommendations in this respect. 
The railway infrastructure manager prepared and implemented a plan for internal inspections. The 
documentation provided shows that, with respect to the supervision of operations at the SPRT depot, the 
only inspections performed covered the completeness and validity of documents confirming the 
qualifications of draisine drivers, as well as the validity of records relating to technical inspections of 
railway vehicles carried out at the depot. None of the audits involved verifying the validity of the 
provisions of the SPRT Technical Regulations. Furthermore, the audits verifying the provisions of the 
Wrocław Nadodrze Technical Regulations, in relation to the supervision of operations at the Wrocław 
Nadodrze station, did not reveal any outdated provisions of the regulations concerning the functioning of 
the SPRT depot, despite this area being included within the scope of the individual audits. Nor did the 
inspections cover the manner in which shunting movements were carried out whilst moving shunting trains 
within the Wrocław Nadodrze station, or the manner in which employees of external entities (e.g. 
employees of the Wolf Security Agency) had to move around the SPRT depot, even though the scope of 
the inspections included the verification of work discipline and the supervision of the safety of railway 
operations at the Wrocław Nadodrze station. The Investigation Team considered this circumstance to be a 
contributing factor to the occurrence.

## 3. Human factors 
### 3.1. Human and individual characteristics 
The driver of the shunting movement’s traction unit was an experienced employee. He had 41 years’ 
service as a driver. He held a valid medical certificate and the required documents confirming his 
qualifications. Sobriety tests conducted by the police at the scene of the accident showed that he was under 
the influence of alcohol at the time of the occurrence. Whilst performing shunting operations, he 
communicated incorrectly with the shunting foreman, with the result that he set the shunting movement in 
motion despite the shunting operation not having been properly discussed, the absence of a signal 
authorising the start of the shunting manoeuvre, and without ensuring that there were no obstacles to 
carrying out the manoeuvre. Although the shunting foreman was not in position on the first wagon being 
pushed, the driver proceeded with the shunting manoeuvre at a maximum speed of 15 km/h. 
The shunting foreman, employed as a manager of service and work trains, was an employee with 28 years’ 
service. He did not hold a valid medical certificate for the post he held. On the day of the accident, he 
failed to properly discuss the shunting operation with the driver, did not take up the required position at 
the front of the shunting movement being pushed, and did not ensure that there were no obstacles 
preventing the start of the shunting manoeuvre. Despite failing to give the driver the signal authorising the 
shunting manoeuvre, he did not react when the driver set the shunting movement in motion, nor did he 
properly monitor the shunting movement’s route. 
Of the staff involved in the occurrence, only the driver was tested for sobriety.

Furthermore, there were two staff members in the cab of the traction vehicle pushing the shunting 
movement who were undergoing training for their driver’s certificate. Their role was to observe the 
driver’s work and had no bearing on the course of the accident. 
After opening the entrance gate to the SPRT depot, a security agency employee turned away from the

The employer stated that the security staff assigned to execute the Contract had been briefed on the health

authorisations and qualifications, up-to-date medical examinations and occupational health and safety 
(OHS) training, had been provided with work clothing, footwear and personal protective equipment, and 
had been made aware of the occupational risks associated with their work. The scope of the training is 
unknown, including whether it covered the risks arising from staff moving around railway premises and 
from working on active tracks. The contract concluded between PKP PLK S.A. and the Wolf Security 
Agency did not provide for security staff to move between the rails of active tracks or to operate the entry 
gates to the SPRT depot. 
The security guard was moving between the rails of active tracks despite having a Group II disability 
(significant visual impairment). The employer presented a medical certificate following periodic medical 
examinations for the post of security guard, issued by a general occupational health service. The post held 
by the victim of the serious accident is not covered by the Regulation of the Minister of Infrastructure of 
11 January 2021 on employees working in posts directly related to the operation and safety of rail traffic 
and to the operation of specific types of railway vehicles, which means that an employee who did not 
possess health qualifications equivalent to those required for railway posts was carrying out duties on 
active railway tracks. 
### 3.2. Job factors  
The DH-350.11-15 railway vehicle was authorised for operation on the Polish railway network. On the 
day of the accident, this vehicle was pushing a shunting movement comprising three type 4Z two-axle flat 
wagons, the first of which, in the direction of travel, was loaded with a technical container, whilst the 
second, also in the direction of travel, was loaded with a Waryński backhoe loader. These conditions meant 
that the driver had no visibility of the track ahead; hence, it was particularly important for the shunting 
foreman to occupy the required position at the front of the shunting movement being pushed. 
On the day of the accident, the security guard was not wearing protective clothing, namely a high-visibility 
vest, or safety footwear. The employee was performing tasks arising from the contract concluded between 
PKP PLK S.A. and the Wolf Security Agency. Under this contract, security staff were not responsible for 
operating the entry gates at the SPRT depot. These duties were performed voluntarily in the spirit of good 
cooperation with PKP PLK S.A. 
### 3.3. Organisational factors and assignments 
The rules governing shunting operations at the SPRT depot were not set out in the Wrocław Nadodrze 
Technical Regulations or in the SPRT Technical Regulations. Instead, the general rules for shunting 
operations set out in instruction Ir-9 applied. Consequently, with respect to the organisation of work at the 
SPRT depot, PKP PLK S.A. had failed, amongst other things, to specify detailed rules for notifying the 
SPRT depot about the approach of the shunting movement and had not designated persons responsible for 
opening and closing the entrance gates. Nor were rules laid down for communication between PKP PLK 
S.A. staff and the security personnel supervising the SPRT depot. Such communication took place via 
mobile phones, and the timing of calls was left to the discretion of the individuals involved. The Wrocław 
Nadodrze Technical Regulations and the SPRT Technical Regulations did not cover all the tracks forming 
part of the depot. Despite carrying out internal audits, the infrastructure manager failed to identify this 
irregularity and did not update the aforementioned documents. The Wrocław Nadodrze Technical 
Regulations were only updated after the accident, on 10 October 2025 and on 24 October 2025. Following 
the update, this document did include all the tracks forming part of the SPRT depot, but it still did not 
specify the rules for performing shunting operations within the depot, the rules for communication between 

## Página 23

staff carrying out activities within the depot, nor did it designate staff to operate the entrance gates. No 
update to the SPRT Technical Regulations was presented. The internal audits conducted by PKP PLK S.A. 
did not cover the manner in which shunting operations were carried out within the SPRT depot (despite 
the scope of certain audits covering the rules for the safe management of rail traffic at Wrocław Nadodrze 
station) or work discipline within the depot, including the use of personal protective equipment by PKP 
PLK S.A. staff and subcontractor staff. PKP PLK S.A. did not monitor the contractor’s performance of the 
Contract, despite having such powers enshrined in the Contract. The Contract did not stipulate an 
obligation for Wolf Security Agency’s employees to operate the entrance gates; rather, these tasks were 
carried out by them in the spirit of good cooperation. 
The investigation team identified the improper organisation of shunting operations at the SPRT depot and 
the security guard’s failure to exercise due care whilst working on active tracks as factors contributing to 
the accident.

PKP PLK S.A. has developed, as part of its safety management system, and applies procedure SMS-PW-
12 ‘Cooperation with suppliers and contractors’. The investigation team has no reservations regarding the 
manner in which the aforementioned procedure was implemented in the context of the occurrence in

PKP PLK S.A. has drawn up and applies procedure SMS-PW-08 as part of its safety management system. 
Paragraph 8 of the aforementioned procedure, in the chapter concerning the recruitment process, specifies 
the scope of medical examinations required, according to general regulations, for individual railway posts. 
Point 3 of this paragraph states that ‘the organisational unit responsible for personnel matters determines 
the type of medical examinations for an employee or job applicant by issuing a referral for initial medical 
examinations and psychological assessments of professional suitability’. The procedure does not specify 
which unit is responsible for issuing referrals and supervising examinations in the case of periodic medical 
examinations, nor for ensuring the continuity of such examinations and the correctness of the referrals 
issued in relation to the post in which the employee is employed. In the occurrence under review, the 
foreman of a service and work train held a medical certificate authorising him to perform duties as a 
mechanic and maintenance technician for rail vehicles until 16 January 2027, following examinations 
conducted on 16 January 2025. The certificate stated that the employee should wear hearing protection 
whilst working in noisy environments. The certificate was issued by an occupational medicine physician at 
the Railway Occupational Health Centre in Wrocław based on a referral for periodic medical examinations 
dated 27 December 2024 for the post of mechanic and rail vehicle maintenance technician, drawn up by 
PKP PLK S.A., Railway Line Plant in Wrocław, Wrocław Główny Operations Section, Deputy Head of 
the Traffic Engineering Section. The referral did not specify that the employee will perform duties as a 
foreman of service and work trains. In the section of the referral concerning the description of working 
conditions and the presence at the workplace of factors that are dangerous or harmful to health, as well as 
factors causing discomfort, it was not stated that the employee will perform, amongst other things, work on 
active tracks, work on the overhead contact line or work on surfaces at different levels. No medical 
certificate authorising the employee to perform the duties as a foreman of service and work trains was 
provided. Despite this, on 31 March 2025, authorisation No. 235/2025 to perform the duties as a foreman 
of service and work trains was issued.

procedure entitled ‘Monitoring and continuous improvement of the Safety Management System and the

#  CONCLUSIONS 
## 1. Summary of the analysis and conclusions about the causes of the occurrence 
The analysis carried out shows that the accident was caused by the following circumstances: 
 
a failure by a Wolf Security Agency employee to comply with the rules for working safely on railway 
premises, including a failure to use personal protective equipment, 
 
allowing, by the Wolf Security Agency, employees to perform activities on active tracks involving the 
operation of entry gates for rolling stock at the SPRT depot, despite the absence of medical 
examinations covering factors arising from working on active railway tracks,  
 
a lack of supervision by the Wolf Security Agency over its employees regarding the execution of the 
contract with PKP PLK S.A. concerning the provision of physical security services at the SPRT depot, 
 
a malfunctioning safety management system of the infrastructure manager PKP PLK S.A. with respect 
to monitoring the continuity of staff medical examinations, the up-to-date nature of the regulations 
governing the organisation of work at the SPRT depot, the supervision of contractors under agreements 
concluded by PKP PLK S.A., and an ineffective system of internal controls, which failed to detect 
irregularities related to the incorrect organisation of shunting operations at the SPRT depot and the 
incorrect performance of shunting work.  
The Investigation Team identified the following as the causal factor: 
 
A shunting movement colliding with a security guard moving between the rails of track No. 209 at 
turnout No. 205. 
The Investigation Team found the contributing factors to be: 
 
Walking between the rails (on the track center line) by the SPRT depot security guard after the entrance 
gate has been opened.  
 
Movement of the security guard in a manner inconsistent with the rules for safe movement on active 
tracks and failure to use personal protective equipment. 
 
Failure to draw up detailed rules for conducting shunting operations at the SPRT depot. 
 
Inadequate briefing on shunting operations by the shunting foreman. 
 
Failure by the shunting foreman to occupy the correct position on the shunting movement whilst 
executing shunting manoeuvres, i.e. in the draisine's cab rather than on the first wagon being pushed. 
 
Start of the shunting movement by the draisine driver despite the shunting foreman not having given 
the signal to commence shunting. 
 
Commencing of entry into the SPRT depot by the shunting movement despite the shunting foreman 
occupying an incorrect position on the shunting movement (in the draisine cab) and the presence of an 
obstruction on the track (a security guard walking between rails). 
 
Performing the duties of the shunting movement driver whilst under the influence of alcohol. 
 
Employing a security guard on active tracks despite a medical certificate of disability and the absence 
of medical examinations required for working on active tracks. 
 
Performing activities involving the opening of entry gates for rolling stock by security staff at the 
SPRT depot, despite the absence of provisions in the contract concluded between the security agency 
and PKP Polskie Linie Kolejowe S.A. which would impose such an obligation on the contractor. 
 
Failure to designate safe access routes to the entry gates for personnel responsible for opening these 
gates for rolling stock entering the SPRT depot. 
 
Failure to exercise proper supervision over security staff operating within the SPRT depot premises. 
The investigation team identified the following as systemic factors: 
 
Employing a person in a post directly related to the operation of rail traffic, who was performing the 
duties of a service and work train manager, without the medical examinations required for that post, 
and issuing authorisation to carry out official duties. 
 
Inadequate supervision under the safety management system of the infrastructure manager PKP PLK 
S.A.:

## 2. Measures taken since the occurrence 
Following the serious accident at the SPRT depot, PKP PLK S.A., Railway Line Plant in Wrocław, 
immediately began to rectify the irregularities identified during the PIP inspection, which was conducted in 
parallel with the Commission’s Investigation Team’s proceedings. These measures included: 
 
updating the Wrocław Nadodrze Technical Regulations; 
 
updating the SPRT Organisational and Technical Regulations, including the addition of the missing 
group of railway tracks to the depot diagram; 
 
marking out safe access routes to the entry gates for rolling stock; 
 
rectifying faults in the train information system at Wrocław Popowice station, 
 
introducing, in the new contract with the Wolf Security Agency, which comes into force on 19 January 
2026, provisions regarding the obligation for security staff to wear high-visibility vests whilst moving 
on railway tracks, opening and closing entry gates, and familiarising themselves with, amongst other 
things, the internal legal acts of PKP Polskie Linie Kolejowe S.A.

#  SAFETY RECOMMENDATIONS 
### 1. Recommendations arising from the investigation: 
1) 
Authorised infrastructure managers, siding users and infrastructure managers exempt from the requirement 
to hold a safety authorisation and operating based on a safety certificate shall review the provisions of 
station technical regulations, special regulations, railway sidings’ operating regulations and other internal 
regulations, with a view to designating and describing safe access routes to all locations where work is 
performed by staff moving within the railway premises. 
2) 
Authorised infrastructure managers, siding users and infrastructure managers exempt from the requirement 
to hold a safety authorisation and operating based on a safety certificate shall ensure that station technical 
regulations, railway siding operating regulations and other special regulations are kept up to date, and that 
their provisions correspond to the actual situation. 
3) 
Authorised infrastructure managers, siding users and infrastructure managers exempt from the requirement 
to hold a safety authorisation and operating based on a safety certificate shall strengthen supervision over 
the manner in which the infrastructure manager’s staff perform shunting operations, including the briefing 
on shunting operations, taking up the correct position on the shunting movement, the correct use of shunting 
signals and monitoring the route of travel. 
4) 
PKP PLK S.A. shall strengthen its supervision of compliance with the provisions of Instruction Ik-3 
regarding checks on the sobriety of staff and the presence of substances with effects similar to alcohol, 
particularly those involved in the operation of railway vehicles. 
5) 
PKP PLK S.A. will strengthen its supervision of the process for authorising staff to work, with respect to 
the validity of medical examinations. 
6) 
Authorised infrastructure managers, siding users and infrastructure managers exempt from the obligation 
to hold a safety authorisation and operating based on a safety certificate shall strengthen supervision over 
contractors performing work on their premises to eliminate cases of movement on active tracks by 
contractors’ employees who do not hold the required authorisations, medical examinations and training 
covering the principles of safe work on and the hazards present on the premises managed by these entities, 
according to their internal regulations. 
7) 
PKP PLK S.A. shall review the risk register and the procedures applied under the safety management 
system with respect to: 
a. the adequacy of the risk control measures applied to specific hazards, 
b. updating the risk register to include hazards arising from ineffective internal controls over the railway 
operations process, 
c. the accuracy of the description of the personnel management process regarding the supervision of the 
continuity of medical examinations for specific job roles. 
8) 
During periodic briefings, PKP PLK S.A. shall remind employees of: 
a. the rules for notifying the Emergency Call Centre (emergency number 112), according to instruction 
Ir-8, 
b. the rules for performing control braking on trains set in motion by the infrastructure manager, 
c. the rules for shunting operations conducted by the infrastructure manager’s staff, including guidance 
on occupying the correct position on the shunting movement, correctly signalling shunting manoeuvres 
and observing the route of travel.
