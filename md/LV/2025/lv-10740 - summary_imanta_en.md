## 1. Summary 
The railway traffic accident (hereinafter – the accident) occurred on 1 October 2025 at 18:00 
during the movement of rolling stock at railway level crossing No. 910 near Imanta stopping point 
(hereinafter – the stopping point) on the Zasulauks–Priedaine railway section. Two underage girls 
were fatally injured in the accident when, while crossing the railway level crossing on an electric 
scooter, they came under passenger train No. 6432M and sustained fatal injuries. 
Protected railway level crossing No. 910 is equipped with automatic level crossing 
signalling, which activates stop signals towards the road, and the automatic barriers assume the 
closed position sufficiently in advance of the arrival of rolling stock to allow road vehicles to clear 
the crossing. The automatic level crossing signalling continues to operate and the automatic 
barriers remain in the closed position until the rolling stock has completely cleared the crossing. 
At the time of the accident, the light signalling and barriers were operating, while a malfunction 
of the acoustic signalling had been identified on one side; no causal link between that malfunction 
and the accident was established. Signs reading “Beware of train” were placed at the crossing in 
clearly visible locations, and an equivalent inscription was also marked on the asphalt of the 
pedestrian path (see Figure 1).

On the day of the accident, the protected level crossing was attended by a level crossing 
keeper of the Track Department of the railway infrastructure manager (LDZ). While performing 
her duties, the level crossing keeper was awaiting passenger train No. 6432M approaching from 
the Jūrmala direction, standing facing the approaching train. As the train approached the railway 
level crossing, the level crossing keeper noticed the girls travelling at high speed on an electric 
scooter along the pedestrian path on Zolitūdes iela from the Jūrmalas gatve side and crossing the 
level crossing. The crossing was closed and an acoustic signal was sounding. The level crossing

keeper gave a warning signal with a signal horn, but the girls did not react and continued moving. 
While continuing to cross the level crossing, the girls entered the path of passenger train No. 
6432M, resulting in a collision with the train. Following the collision, the electric train stopped 
beyond the level crossing area. 
Passenger train No. 6432M (electric multiple unit 16Ev No. 0011, manufactured in 2023 at 
the “Škoda Vagonka a.s.” plant) was operated by a traction unit driver (train driver) (hereinafter – 
the electric train driver) working alone. Before Imanta stopping point, train No. 6432M was 
carrying 103 passengers. A scheduled stop was planned at this stopping point. When approaching 
Imanta stopping point, the electric train driver commenced service braking at a train speed of 99 
km/h and, at the location specified by the railway infrastructure, sounded a warning acoustic signal 
using the low-volume whistle. The electric train driver then noticed two persons on an electric 
scooter crossing the railway level crossing via the pedestrian path without stopping, while the 
crossing was closed to road vehicles and pedestrians, and not reacting to the acoustic warning 
signal. The electric train driver sounded the high-volume warning signal (horn) and applied 
emergency braking at a train speed of 75 km/h, however, the collision could not be prevented. 
### Causal factor 
The injured persons entered the danger zone of the railway level crossing and crossed it 
while the level crossing light and acoustic signalling prohibited crossing and passenger train No. 
6432M was approaching the level crossing. 
### Contributing factors 
• The crossing area of the railway level crossing was not equipped with physical measures 
requiring users to change their movement trajectory or reduce their speed, thereby 
encouraging assessment of the situation and verification, before crossing the tracks, that no 
train was approaching; 
• Limited visibility of the approaching train from the approach to the level crossing, which 
reduced the possibility of visually perceiving the approaching train in sufficient time; 
• At the time of the accident, the access-control measures of the shared electric scooter rental 
service did not ensure effective compliance with the age restriction established by the 
Lessor, as a result of which the injured persons were able to obtain access to the shared 
vehicle used in the accident, although the Lessor’s rules prohibited its use by persons under 
18 years of age. 
### Systemic factor 
The legislation in force at the time of the accident did not establish specific requirements for 
the minimum safety equipment of railway pedestrian crossings depending on train speed, train 
visibility and pedestrian traffic intensity. 
The causal and contributing factors identified during the investigation describe the accident 
mechanism and the functioning of the safety system and shall not be regarded as a determination 
of personal fault or legal liability. 
As a result of the investigation, three safety recommendations were developed. 
The safety recommendations concern: 
• improvement of systematic inspections of railway level crossings and assessment of pedestrian 
safety equipment; 
• assessment of the need for permanent video surveillance at the Imanta railway level crossing; 
• implementation of the planned engineering safety measures and assessment of their 
effectiveness in order to reduce the risk of unauthorised and unsafe track crossing and prevent 
the recurrence of similar accidents.

5.a. Summary of the analysis and conclusions with regard to the causes of the occurrence 
The investigation identified the following factors affecting the occurrence: 
### Causal factor 
The injured persons entered the danger zone of the railway level crossing and crossed it 
while the level crossing light and acoustic signalling prohibited crossing and passenger train No. 
6432M was approaching the level crossing.

• The crossing area of the railway level crossing was not equipped with physical measures 
requiring users to change their movement trajectory or reduce their speed, thereby 
encouraging assessment of the situation and verification, before crossing the tracks, that no 
train was approaching;

• At the time of the accident, the access-control measures of the shared electric scooter

of personal fault or legal liability.

### Changes to the legal framework for electric micromobility vehicles 
Following the accident at the Imanta railway level crossing on 1 October 2025, the issue of 
the classification of electric micromobility vehicles and the conditions for their use in road traffic, 
including in relation to minors, was brought into focus.

On 6 November 2025, amendments to the Road Traffic Law were adopted, clarifying the 
definition of a bicycle and introducing separate terms “electric bicycle” and “self-propelled 
bicycle”. The amendments distinguished types of electric bicycles according to electric motor 
power and operating principle and established additional requirements for their use. 
At the same time, it was established that persons under 14 years of age are prohibited from 
participating in road traffic with an electric bicycle, self-propelled bicycle or electric scooter, while 
persons aged 14 and over must hold the right to drive a bicycle or a vehicle of any category. 
Additional requirements were also established for shared-vehicle service providers concerning 
verification of the user’s age and driving entitlement. 
The relevant provisions of the Road Traffic Law concerning the classification of electric 
bicycles and self-propelled bicycles entered into force on 1 February 2026. 
On 24 March 2026, amendments to Cabinet Regulation No. 279 of 2 June 2015 “Road 
Traffic Regulations” were adopted, specifying the requirements for the use of bicycles, self-
propelled bicycles and other micromobility vehicles in road traffic. 
At the same time, following the accident, the need to improve safety requirements for railway 
level crossings and pedestrian crossings themselves, including their construction, equipment and 
safety assessment, was also brought into focus. During the investigation it was established that the 
Ministry of Transport had developed a draft Cabinet Regulation “Amendments to Cabinet 
Regulation No. 392 of 6 October 1998 ‘Regulations regarding the Construction, Equipment, 
Servicing and Closure of Railway Level Crossings and Pedestrian Crossings’”. The annotation to 
the draft states that the need for the amendments was also prompted by the tragedy that occurred 
at the Zolitūdes iela railway level crossing on 1 October 2025. 
The planned changes include stricter requirements for the equipment of level crossings and 
pedestrian crossings, more detailed classification thereof, regular safety inspections, clearer 
allocation of responsibilities between the parties involved, restrictions on the construction of new 
level crossings, and gradual adaptation of the infrastructure to the new requirements by 2037 (see 
Table 1). These measures are aimed at improving the safety of road users and reducing the risk of 
similar accidents in the future.

### Measures taken by the Consumer Rights Protection Centre (CRPC) 
Following the accident of 1 October 2025, the Consumer Rights Protection Centre (CRPC) 
initiated supervision measures concerning the micromobility vehicle rental service provided by 
the Lessor. On 2 October 2025, the CRPC required the immediate suspension of the service and 
submission of evidence confirming its suspension. Subsequently, the service provider’s 
explanations were assessed, checks of the operation of the service were carried out and, on 9 and 
13 October 2025, a risk assessment of the service was organised with the participation of 
representatives of the CRPC, the State Police, State JSC “Road Traffic Safety Directorate”, Riga 
State City Municipality and the Children’s Clinical University Hospital. The risk assessment 
concluded that the service posed a serious risk because minors were able to use the rental service 
without effective identity and age verification. 
On the basis of the risk assessment, on 20 October 2025 the CRPC adopted a decision 
ordering the Lessor to discontinue provision of the service until effective verification of the 
identity and age of service recipients had been introduced and the service had been made 
unavailable to persons under 18 years of age. At the same time, the CRPC established additional 
safety requirements in the event that the service were to be provided to minors in the future, 
including requirements concerning minimum age, driving entitlement, personal protective 
equipment and consent of legal representatives. 
According to information provided by the CRPC on 9 July 2026, the Lessor subsequently 
introduced an identity and age verification solution using document and biometric data 
verification, which the CRPC recognised as sufficient to eliminate the serious risk. The CRPC also 
carried out mystery-shopping checks, which established that minors using their own identity 
documents could no longer gain access to the rental service. Certain cases identified later were 
related to unauthorised use of third-party identity data rather than deficiencies in the Lessor’s 
control mechanism. 
### Safety measures implemented and planned by Limited Liability Company “RIDE” 
In connection with the accident of 1 October 2025, the Investigation Bureau contacted the 
shared micromobility service provider (the Lessor), requesting information on measures 
implemented and planned by the company to reduce the risk of similar accidents. 
The Investigation Bureau specifically requested information on measures for restricting 
minors’ access to the service, ensuring and monitoring compliance with the prohibition on several 
persons simultaneously using one vehicle, as well as on other safety improvements implemented 
or planned after the accident and cooperation with State, municipal or other institutions. 
 Restriction of access by minors 
The Lessor informed that the company’s services may be used only by persons who have 
reached the specified age and comply with the requirements laid down in legislation. 
To restrict minors’ access to the service, the company has introduced user identity and age 
verification. Before starting a ride, the customer must complete verification by submitting an 
identity document confirming that the person is at least 18 years of age. Where breaches of the 
terms of use are identified, the company may block or restrict the respective user account.

In addition, the Lessor informs users in the application about the safety requirements for 
using the service and the restrictions laid down in legislation. 
 Restriction on several persons travelling on one vehicle 
The Lessor’s terms of use prohibit several persons from travelling on one vehicle at the same 
time. 
To reduce the risk of such infringements, the company displays safety rules and warnings in 
the application, and information stickers are placed on the self-propelled bicycles stating that two 
persons are prohibited from travelling on one vehicle at the same time. Where breaches of the 
terms of use are identified, the company may block or restrict the user account. 
At the same time, the Lessor indicated that complete control of such infringements by 
technological means in the field of shared micromobility services is difficult. In the company’s 
view, public education and road-safety campaigns also play an important role. The Lessor also 
indicated that, in practice, enforcement of prohibitions laid down in road traffic regulations falls 
within the competence of law-enforcement authorities, including the State Police and municipal 
police. 
 Other safety improvements and initiatives 
After the accident, the Lessor additionally assessed possible measures for improving the 
safety of shared micromobility services. 
As directions for further safety measures, the company identified enhanced information to 
users on safety requirements in the vicinity of railway level crossings and other high-risk locations, 
cooperation with State and municipal institutions on road-safety matters, and analysis of safety 
data and incidents in order to identify potential risks and improve preventive measures. 
The Lessor confirmed its readiness to continue cooperating with the responsible institutions 
in order to promote traffic safety and reduce the risk of similar accidents. 
### 5.c. Additional observations 
Following the railway traffic accident that occurred on 1 October 2025 at 18:00 during the 
movement of rolling stock at railway level crossing No. 910 near Imanta stopping point on the 
Zasulauks–Priedaine railway section, on the same day, 01.10.2025 at 20:39 on the Sloka–Ķemeri 
section, km 40 pk 3, the same electric train driver of passenger train No. 6534M (electric multiple 
unit 16Ev-0011) applied emergency braking in order to avoid striking a person (a woman). The 
collision could not be avoided; the woman was struck and sustained an injury to her arm. The 
police and emergency medical personnel were called. The following trains were delayed: No. 
6534M – 1 h 19 min; No. 6531 – 1 h 05 min; No. 6536 – 1 h 12 min; No. 6533 – 1 h 07 min; No. 
6538 – 1 h 05 min; No. 6535 – 51 min; No. 6537 – 27 min. 
At the time of the accident, the Railway Undertaking’s internal rules did not establish a 
specific procedure for replacing a train driver in cases where a train had struck a person, animal, 
object or vehicle, etc. Following any accident, the responsible (duty) driver instructor conducts a 
conversation with the train driver in order to determine the train driver’s psychological condition 
and ability to continue the journey. If the train driver reports that he or she cannot continue driving 
the train, replacement of that train driver is organised immediately and, in parallel, passenger 
transport by alternative means is arranged. If, however, the train driver states that he or she is able 
to continue the journey, replacement is organised in a planned manner in Riga or at the turnaround 
point. 
Following the accident under investigation, the electric train driver informed the driver 
instructor that he felt normal and would continue the journey. Taking into account the seriousness 
of the accident involving train No. 6432M at railway level crossing No. 910, the Railway

During the investigation, the Railway Undertaking reached an agreement with the Latvian 
Railway and Transport Industry Trade Union on amendments to the Collective Agreement 
concerning specific actions to be taken when a person has been struck. The Collective Agreement 
was supplemented with the following provisions: 
• replacement is ensured as soon as possible, while remuneration is retained until the end 
of the working period (tour of duty); 
• three days of rest are granted from the day following the end of the working period 
(tour of duty) during which the collision occurred, while average earnings are retained; 
• initial psychological assistance is provided within 72 hours after the occurrence; 
• on the basis of an employee’s application, the Railway Undertaking sends the employee 
for rehabilitation as soon as possible. Average earnings are retained for the period spent at the 
rehabilitation centre, but not longer than the period covered by an A-type certificate of 
incapacity for work. 
The Railway Undertaking’s internal procedure was improved, namely the “Process 
Description for Organising the Work of Traction Rolling Stock Locomotive Crews” was approved 
(by JSC “Pasažieru vilciens” Order No. 34-2026 of 06.02.2026), which also provides that, in cases 
where a person has been struck or a collision has occurred resulting in injury to a person, 
replacement of the locomotive crew must be organised as soon as possible. The locomotive crew

## 6. Safety Recommendations 
### Safety Recommendation 2026-2* 
State joint-stock company “Latvijas dzelzceļš” should develop a uniform checklist for 
annual inspections of railway level crossings, also including criteria for assessing equipment and 
infrastructure intended for the safety of pedestrians, cyclists and users of micromobility devices. 
Justification: Use of a uniform checklist will promote a systematic and consistent approach 
to railway level crossing safety assessment, completeness of inspections and timely identification 
of safety deficiencies and necessary improvements. 
### Safety Recommendation 2026-3 
State joint-stock company “Latvijas dzelzceļš” should assess the need to install a permanent 
video-surveillance system at the Imanta railway level crossing in order to obtain objective 
information on the level crossing and the adjacent pedestrian movement area, identify possible 
unsafe behaviour and assess the effectiveness of the safety measures implemented. 
Justification: The purpose of the video-surveillance system is to improve monitoring of 
traffic-safety risks, provide objective information for the investigation of accidents and incidents, 
identify trends in dangerous behaviour by road users and assess the effectiveness of the safety 
measures introduced, enabling necessary safety measures to be taken in a timely manner and 
thereby contributing to the prevention of similar accidents in the future. 
### Safety Recommendation 2026-4 
State joint-stock company “Latvijas dzelzceļš” should implement the engineering safety 
measures provided for in the project at the Imanta railway level crossing by constructing fences at 
both approaches to the level crossing in order to restrict the possibility of bypassing the labyrinth-
type barrier and accessing the railway tracks outside the designated pedestrian crossing. 
Justification: The existing solution does not restrict the possibility of bypassing the 
labyrinth-type barrier and accessing the railway tracks outside the designated pedestrian crossing 
point. 
* The numbering of safety recommendations here begins with the second recommendation. The first recommendation 
in 2026 was developed by the Investigation Bureau in the course of investigating another accident.
