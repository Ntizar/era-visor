1 Summary 
The first section contains a brief description of the event, as well as information on the 
consequences, primary causes and safety recommendations provided in the individual case. 
1.1 Brief description of the event 
On 27.07.2025 around 6:08 p.m. the passenger train RE 3227 on the journey from Sigmaringen 
to Ulm at the height of the village Riedlingen-Zell collided with deposits of a hillslope debris 
flow and derailed with almost all axes. 
1.2 Consequences 
The leading vehicle slid uphill to the left in the direction of travel and was destroyed by another 
collision with trees standing there. Three people were killed, five were seriously injured and 
31 were slightly injured. Damage to the vehicles and infrastructure amounted to 
approximately €10 million.

# 5 Conclusions 
The following section contains a summary of the identified causal, contributing and systemic 
factors. In addition, two further subsections are provided containing information about 
measures already taken, and additional comments 
### 5.1 Summary and conclusion 
The event was caused by the loss of the RE 3227 [F3] track guidance following a collision with 
a debris flow [F2] that fell into the track area and was triggered by a heavy rain event [F1]. 
### The causal factor ‘heavy rain event’ [F1] 
According to the German Weather Service, the regionally limited short-term heavy rain event 
was not an extremely unusual event. Rainfall at a similar level and duration has been recorded 
repeatedly in the area since 2001 and therefore cannot be classified as exceptional. 
Accordingly, the projected values for the operational control centres of the infrastructure 
manager and the railway undertaking did not give rise to the imposition of measures under 
Directive 420. The limits of intervention set within each company were not exceeded. 
The local signalers had perceived the thunderstorm front as disturbing, but had not yet seen 
any reason to take protective measures under Directive 408.0541 on their own responsibility. 
The conflict between safety and punctuality described in chapter 4.3.1 may have influenced 
the decision-making of local personnel. Point 4.2.1 of Annex II to Regulation (EU) 2018/762 
requires the competence management system of the organisation to ensure that staff with 
safety-related tasks are qualified to perform the safety-related tasks under its responsibility.  
DB InfraGO AG's resilience strategy is intended to develop further specifications in order to 
relieve operating employees of the burden of assessing dangerous circumstances. 
### The causal factor ‘hillslope debris flow’ [F2] 
The event site was identified as a potentially risky site in both the hazard mapping of the 
German Centre for Rail Research (DZSF) and the heavy rain scenarios of the city of Riedlingen, 
albeit with a ‘small’ hazard potential. It could be assumed that these theoretically determined 
risks were neither known nor made known to the plant managers of DB InfraGO AG. In this 
respect, no increased attention was paid to the embankment area covered by the hillslope 
debris flow. As part of the resilience strategy, risks will be mapped by means of simulation-
theoretical approaches in the future in order to be able to take preventive measures with the

The condition of the municipal road drainage systems above the railway embankment was not

### The causal factor ‘Loss of lane guidance’ [F3] 
The debris flow in the track caused by the mud drain were sufficient in the present 
constellation to interrupt the safe track guidance of the leading vehicle of the RE3227. It is 
very likely that the high speed of the tilting train in combination with the rounded design of 
the lower front aprons during the collision with the debris flow favoured the lifting of the 
leading bogie and triggered the derailment. The existing design of the track clearer was not 
suitable for draining viscous sludge and soil in front of the bogie. The technical requirements 
for levellers have already been further developed. 
The dismantling of the high kinetic energy of the train formation caused the massive 
destruction of the leading vehicle during the subsequent impact on the trees next to the track. 
A lower speed would have, with a certain probability, either prevented the derailment process 
or at least mitigated its consequences. From the point of view of safe operation, a preventive 
step-by-step reduction of the maximum permissible speeds of trains, if necessary, could also 
reduce the risk of similar events in certain series in the event of heavy rain or other severe 
weather conditions.  
conclusion 
The departure of the debris flow was not foreseeable for the directly involved operating 
personnel. The higher-level operations control centres of the IM and the RU did not identify 
any need for action with regard to preventive measures to ensure operational safety after the 
announced weather events. IM plant managers also had no indication of the instability of the 
slope. At the same time, the embankment was already classified as potentially risky in 
simulation-theoretical considerations. These findings have not yet been fully incorporated 
into the railway undertakings’ resilience strategy. There are already more detailed instructions 
for mitigating risks in the event of a storm. Further instructions for action to avert dangers 
from other climate-related weather influences are still in the planning and implementation 
phase. The measures outlined in the IM’s resilience strategy represent an already strategic

### 5.2 Measures taken since the event 
In addition to the repair of the damaged railway facilities and drainage components, the upper 
and deep drainage in the relevant section of the line was upgraded by DB InfraGO AG. 
Together with the city of Riedlingen, protective measures were taken for the permanent 
hydraulic separation of the railway body from draining surface water. These include, among 
other things, a concrete latch above the web incision on both sides of the bridge in order to 
keep surface water from the incision and a controlled discharge of this surface water outside 
the railway facilities. In addition, slope and erosion protection measures were carried out to 
stabilize the embankment on both sides of the road bridge.  
The resilience strategy of DB InfraGO AG, which is already explained in chapter 4.4, serves, 
among other things, the longer-term control of hazards from severe weather events and is 
constantly being further developed. A Policy Department for Natural Hazard Management has 
been established.  
The DZSF is planning further research projects in the report on the Research Strategy 2026 – 
2030 in continuation of previous and ongoing research projects on the impact of climate-
related changes in natural hazards and their impact on the safety of railway operations. On 
the basis of these results, appropriate prevention measures can be developed.
