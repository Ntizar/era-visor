### Overview of the occurrence 
On 10 November 2013, at 15:28 hrs., between the stations Mezőkeresztes-
Mezőnyárád and Mezőkövesd, fire broke out on the locomotive with track no. 91 
53 0480 007-0 hauling the train no. 47221. The engine driver stopped the train at 
Mezőkövesd Felső stop. The fire brigade extinguished the fire. 
The interstation track was closed until 18:20 hrs. 
No personal injury occurred as a consequence of the accident. 
On the day after the date of the accident, the IC performed the primary technical 
survey of the locomotive at the site of MÁV-TRAKCIÓ Ltd. in Hatvan. 
As the locomotive was repaired at the site of the manufacturer, the IC initiated a 
detailed technical examination of the damaged vehicle at the fellow authority 
competent according to the location of the manufacturer’s site. 

## Página 6

The conclusion of the examination performed in Romania was that the fire in the 
locomotive had been caused by the malfunction of the oil cooler equipment of the 
vehicle. 
As a result of the malfunction, and due to the specific array of the major machinery in 
the engine compartment, the transformer oil of the locomotive spilt onto the smoothing 
choke coil, where it accumulated and caught fire. 
Following the comments made during the on-site investigation, the manufacturer of the 
locomotive decided to try to reduce the probability of similar cases by modifying the 
design of the locomotive. 
As the manufacturer of the locomotive fulfilled the request relevant to the technical 
review, the IC finds it unnecessary to issue any safety recommendation in connection 
with the occurrence.

### Course of events 
On the day of the occurrence, the train no. 47221 departed from Hidasnémeti 
station at 13:10 hrs. The train consisted of 28 loaded four-axle wagons. The gross 
total weight of the train was 1,895 tons, and its length from the aspect of braking 
was 391 metres. 
### During the travel of the train, at 15:09 hrs., in the district of Mezőkeresztes-
Mezőnyárád station, the display in the driver’s cab showed an error signal coming 
from the traction motor no. 1 of the locomotive of the train. After the error signal, 
the traction motor was disabled. 
After a short time, the driver tried to enable the traction motor no. 1, but the error 
signal appeared again. After disabling the traction motor again, the main breaker 
of the vehicle released automatically, owing to the integrated protection. 
When it was possible to reset the main breaker again, the driver tried to continue 
traction, but then new error messages appeared on the display, and soon the 
warning signal referring to fire in the engine compartment appeared, too. 
At the time of the appearance of the fire signal, the train was travelling at a speed 
of 47 km/h. The driver stopped the train at the station Mezőkövesd Felső at 15:20 
hrs. 
The notified firefighters arrived soon, and began to extinguish the fire.

### 1.14 
### Tests and research 
On 11 November 2013, after the accident, the IC, together with a representative of 
MMV Ltd., viewed the burnt-out locomotive in Hatvan. 
The following findings were recorded during such survey: 
− 
The worst signs of burn can be seen at the smoothing choke coils 
connected to the main transformer, and at the oil cooler unit situated 
above it. 
− 
As an effect of the heat, the battery pack, the insulation of high-voltage 
cables, and the electronic devices situated near the starting point of the 
fire burnt or melted. 
− 
The entire engine compartment and both driver workstations are 
contaminated with soot and other combustion products. 
− 
Both sides of the body of the locomotive burnt in the middle part. 
− 
It was impossible to read the data in the data recorder of the 
locomotive; the representative of MMV Ltd. agreed to send the 
retrieved data to TSB Hungary. 
− 
The locomotive received its license on 28 October 2013, and ran 
12,043 km till the occurrence in total. 
− 
The upkeeper will transport the vehicle to the manufacturer (Craiova) 
where the necessary repairs will be performed. 
On 14 November 2013, after the transportation of the locomotive to the 
manufacturer’s site, the electrical locomotive was subject to a detailed 
examination at the site of SC SOFTRONIC Craiova in the presence of 
representatives of TSB Hungary, AFER, OIFR, and MMV Ltd. 
The following findings were recorded during such survey: 
− 
The marks of strong heat impact can be seen around the cooler of the 
main transformer (side walls, cooling element, upper covers of the 
locomotive, and the corridors) in the engine compartment. 
− 
The boards S1, S6, S7, S8, ICSA (auxiliary converter) are heavily 
contaminated with soot outside, however, owing to their good 
insulation, the internal parts were found in normal condition. 
− 
The external and internal cables of the transformer and the external 
and internal cables of S7, S1, S6, S8, ICSA, as well as the insulation of 
the electrical connection joint couplings suffered deformation due to the 
heat. 
− 
The active part of the main transformer is intact; the SOFTRONIC 
personnel performed the relevant technical measurements, and the 
data obtained corresponded to the original values. The oil in the 
transformer was clear, not cloudy, and its disruptive strength was within 
the normal range. 
− 
The outer side of the oil tank of the main transformer is smoky, but was 
not deformed by the heat. 
− 
The blades of the cooling-ventilating devices in the cooling unit of the 
transformer melted due to the strong heat, and the radiator elements 
opened up at the joints. 
− 
The heat caused significant deformation to the lower parts of the 
smoothing choke coils of the traction engines 
− 
The rotating parts of the engines (the traction engines integrated in the 
bogies) were not exposed to the effect of heat. 
− 
The batteries in the battery pack suffered serious deformation.

### Rules and regulations 
The fire protection tasks of the engine crew are specified in Instruction E.1: 
### Procedure in the case of fire in a locomotive 
“7.3. In the case of fire or any sign of fire (unusual sparks, smoke, burning smell) the 
locomotive shall be turned off immediately, and the train shall be stopped by emergency 
braking. It is forbidden to stop in a tunnel, on a bridge, and next to another train staying on 
the adjacent tracks on a section with two or more tracks, and within 40 m of a nationwide 
electric grid that crosses the railway; in such areas, the engine driver may use the 
emergency brake bypassing system of the train if available. If the doors are controlled 
centrally then, after stopping the train, the permission of opening of the doors shall be 
given for that side which allows the passengers to get off in a safer manner. If an operable 
loudspeaker system is available such permission shall be communicated to the 
passengers, and they shall also be warned of the risk of accident. 
7.3.1. The starting point and the extension of the fire shall be determined. Endangered 
vehicles and other combustible materials shall be removed from the vicinity of the burning 
locomotive if possible. If the flames may get closer than 2 metres of the overhead wire or 
its accessories the turning off of voltage in such wire shall be initiated. 
7.3.2. The crew of the locomotive shall utilise all means and possibility to extinguish the 
fire, including the use of manual fire extinguishers as required. 
7.3.3. After performing the safety tasks, start to extinguish the fire immediately, using the 
fire extinguisher (equipment) held in place on the locomotive. Water or foam may only be 
used for fire extinguishing on electrical lines only after turning of the overhead wire and 
after earthing…. 
7.4. If the fire in the locomotive started at a station or on the track than the engine crew 
shall notify the station inspector or the line traffic manager immediately. 
7.4.1. If the fire in the locomotive started at a vehicle maintenance site the engine crew 
shall notify the locomotive running foreman or the person specified in the Fire Protection 
Rules of the facility. 
7.4.2. After such notification, the extinguishing of the fire shall be started immediately.” 
The Hungarian version of the manufacturer’s manual, edited by MMV Ltd. for the 
locomotives type LE-MA2 specifies the following in connection with “RELEASES 
OF THE MAIN BREAKER” (Chapter TROUBLESHOOTING ACTIVITIES): 
„A1. Overloading of the 25KV circuit 
The protection triggers often, especially if the line voltage value is high when the 
locomotive is powered up. A short-term disconnection of the pantograph from the 
overhead wire when loaded may be the cause. 
If the operation of the protection causes big noise or smoke in the engine compartment, 
with oil leaking from the transformer, or the typical noise of boiling of oil comes from the 
transformer then IT IS STRICTLY PROHIBITED TO TURN ON THE MAIN BREAKER 
afterwards. 
A2. Either the door of the board of S1… S6, S7, S8 or the doors of the ICSA boards are 
open. 
Check whether the doors and microsensors close properly, and to achieve that, eliminate 
the contact errors of the microsensor by repeated local treatment. 
A3. The sensors of the fire alarm system have been triggered.

### The engine driver’s knowledge of the locomotive type: 
The driver of the locomotive of train no. 47221 obtained the locomotive driving 
licence, which entitles him to drive locomotives type LE-MA2 (480Ro), on 05 
December. The preliminary examination in the knowledge of the vehicle was 
organised and conducted by the Hungarian upkeeper of the locomotive. Such 
examination organised by the railway company is valid for one year, thus the 
engine driver had valid knowledge of the vehicle type on the day of the 
occurrence. 
After the arrival of the new locomotive, MMV Ltd. held a one-day training session 
where the vehicle, the layout of the engine compartment and the location of the 
controls were introduced to the relevant crew. 
The engine driver had served on the affected locomotive on two occasions before. 
The locomotive is driven very much like the Series 610 locomotive which is 
already in service at the railway company, and which the engine driver had already 
been familiar with. The location of the controls, displays as well as the contents of 
their feedback are identical with the type 601 already in service. 
The petition for approval of the type knowledge module of the electrical locomotive 
of the series 480Ro was approved by the Railway Examination Centre of Nonprofit 
Llc. under no. VVK-2013/1371 on 09 September 2013. 
The basic training in the knowledge of the vehicle (based on approved training 
program) for the engine drivers, as well as their examination by the authorities, 
took place after the occurrence. 
### Running of train no. 47221 
The departure station of the train no. 47221 was Hidasnémeti, and its destination 
was Curtici (Kürtös) station. The train consisted of 28 loaded four-axle wagons 
hauled by the locomotive no. 91 53 0480 007-0. 
The train no. 47221 departed from Hidasnémeti station at 13:10 hrs. on the date 
of the occurrence. Nothing extraordinary happened on the way of the train until 
the accident. 
When approaching Mezőkeresztes-Mezőnyárád station, the engine driver 
regulated the speed of the train so that he can stop the train in front of the entry 
signal at danger, because the entry signal of the station was at danger. 
The train was approaching the entry signal at a speed of approx. 30 km/h when 
the entry signal changed to clear (one green light). Concurrently, the EVM display 
situated on the vehicle also started to display “MAX”. Then the engine driver 
turned the combined switch-group operating lever to traction position. As an effect 
of the power uptake, the traction motor no. 1 of the vehicle sent an error message 
to the display of the engine driver’s workstation at 15:09 hrs.

## Página 16

Due to the repeated error item, the engine driver decided to disable the traction 
motor no. 1, so he turned off the main breaker of the vehicle at 15:09 hrs., and, 
using the control unit placed in the driver’s cab, he disabled the traction motor no. 
1. According to the driving data recorded, at that time the train was approaching 
the entry signal of Mezőkeresztes-Mezőnyárád station, travelling at a speed of 29 
km/h. 
After the operation was performed, the speed of the train increased to 60 km/h. 
The train was travelling in the switching zone at the departure side of 
Mezőkeresztes-Mezőnyárád station when the engine driver decided to enable the 
disabled traction motor again, so he turned the main breaker off.  
The main breaker was turned on again between Mezőkeresztes-Mezőnyárád and 
### Mezőkövesd stations, at 15:13 hrs., after passing the phase boundary in Section 
1455. 
In the meantime, the engine driver notified the station inspector of Mezőkövesd 
that he wishes to take an extraordinary stop at the station in order to clarify the 
cause of the error detected. According to the engine driver’s report, he did not 
notice anything unusual when he opened the door of the engine compartment, so 
he did not stop the train but continued the journey. 
However, after the traction was restarted, the error message appeared again on 
the display of the driver’s workstation. Acknowledging the error message, the 
engine driver continued the journey. After about a minute, the screen symbol of 
traction motor no. 3 also showed the same error message which had appeared for 
traction motor no. 1 before. When analysing the data recorded, the IC found that 
in that case also, the error message appeared due to an increase in the Ipm value. 
After about 10 seconds, the protection system of the train triggered, and the main 
breaker released automatically. Now the engine driver decided to disable the 
traction motor no. 1 again.  
Twelve seconds after the repeated reset of the main breaker, the engine driver 
turned the grouped switch to traction position again. One second after starting 
traction, the error message from traction motor no. 3 appeared again, and the 
main breaker triggered again after 6 seconds. 
When the main breaker was fit for resetting again, the engine driver did not reset it 
immediately. The train was rolling without traction effort from the locomotive, and 
its speed decreased from 53 km/h to 48 km/h. As the engine driver intended to get 
the train to the next station, he began to haul the train again 15 seconds after 
resetting the main breaker, at 15:17:56 hrs. 
As an effect of power uptake, several error messages appeared on the display 
relevant to the traction motors 1, 5, 3, and 4 (in the order of appearance). The 
engine driver continued the traction, and, in the meantime, the symbol indicating 
fire also appeared on the display at 15:18:04 hrs. After the appearance of the fire 
icon, the engine driver did not stop traction immediately but maintained it until the 
main breaker of the locomotive triggered again.

When he got back to the engine driver’s workstation he compressed the

It was established during the analysis of the recorded data that 11 minutes and 10

The interval of time between the appearance of the fire alarm and the stop was 2

The driver was able to stop the train in a place which was easy to access for the 
firefighters. Although the train stopped in an inhabited area, the fire burning in the 
vehicle did not cause immediate danger to any third person or property of any

### The engine driver’s activity 
The engine drive was performing his third day of service on the locomotive newly 
purchased by the company. The locomotive represents a higher technological 
level in comparison to the vehicles used by the company before. The output of the 
locomotive is 6000 kW. 
The engine driver, as he said, had perceived the error messages appearing at the 
driver’s workstation in the course of traction, however, he had not clarified the 
origin of such error messages. As he reported, his intent had been to clarify the 
situation and find the cause of the problem after stopping at Mezőkövesd station. 
The total load of the hauled train was 2021 tons. Aware of the output of the 
locomotive, the driver thought that he would be able to reach Mezőkövesd station 
with the train despite having lost one traction motor. This intent is reflected in the 
course of actions he performed after the appearance of the first error signal and 
even after the fire alarm. 
“As usual”, he repeatedly enabled the traction motor no. 1 which had sent the first 
error message, being confident that the error would not occur again. However, it 
did, but he did not disable the traction motor again but continued the traction of 
the train until the main breaker triggered automatically. He did not stop the train 
after the appearance of the fire alarm but even slightly increased the traction 
force, thus trying to reach Mezőkövesd station. 
The Instruction No. E.1 (Section 7.3. herein) stipulates that “In the case of fire or 
any sign of fire (unusual sparks, smoke, burning smell) the locomotive shall be put 
turned off immediately, and the train shall be stopped by emergency braking. 
When the fire alarm appeared, the train was at a distance of 2 kilometres of 
Mezőkövesd town, in an area where the train, if stopped, would have been difficult 
to access by road vehicles, and thus, fire extinguishing would have been subject 
to delay. 
From the aspect of starting fire extinguishing as soon as possible, the actual place 
of stopping of the train was more favourable, as the Fire Brigade of the self-
government of Mezőkövesd – which has its site 2 km of the place where the train

the engine compartment, he would possible have detected early signs of the 
incipient fire. 
### Layout of the transformer of the locomotive 
In operating conditions, the engine compartment of the affected locomotive type is 
subject to overpressure. 
The transformer unit of the locomotive is installed in the middle of the engine 
compartment. Its role is to provide appropriate voltage for the traction motors, 
auxiliary operation, and train heating. 
The one-phase transformer is sunk into a tank filled with oil subject to forced 
circulation. In operating conditions, the tank contains approx. 4000 litres of 
transformer oil. The cooling of the circulating oil is supported by the ventilators of 
the engine. 
Layout of the equipment: the fan, the oil cooler and the house itself, which 
contains the smoothing choke coils in the bottom part, are integrated with one 
another and installed next to the transformer unit. 
The design of the floor of the locomotive provides vertical flow of ventilation air. 
The piping which circulates the cooling oil of the transformer is equipped with a 
manually operated stopping system (butterfly valve) both before and after the oil 
cooler. This manually operated valve was not used after detecting the fire, which 
allowed part of the oil in the transformer tank to leak from the tank via gravity, and 
added more fuel to the fire. 
### Experiences of the technical survey 
The findings of the technical survey performed in Hatvan on the day following the 
date of the accident also confirmed the experiences of the engine driver, namely 
that the starting place of the fire in the locomotive was the area around the 
smoothing choke coils. 
Based on the findings of the survey, the Committee concluded that the root cause 
was the leakage of the cooling elements due to a hidden density problem. A minor 
leakage had developed between the cooling elements, which dilated with time due 
the effect of heat, and made it possible for the oil to leak from the oil cooler and to 
feed the fire. As the starting of heavily loaded freight trains requires high power 
uptake, and such high power uptake induces large heat impact around the 
induction coils, which may be able to start a fire in unfavourable conditions.

### Change in the amperage of the earth leakage current 
After evaluating the data recorded by the on-board equipment of the locomotive 
involved in the occurrence, the IC established that the error messages appearing 
on the display situated at the driver’s workstation were due to sudden changes in 
the amperage of the earth leakage current. 
In response to our request, the experts of SOFTRONIC wrote that the sending of 
the error message indicated that the insulation of the elements of the given 
electrical circuit was damaged by the incipient fire. 
Although the heat sensors installed on the locomotive were near the place where 
the fire started, they triggered only later on, as their response time was longer, 
because the inner temperature of the engine compartment did not increase 
suddenly, due to the design of ventilation.

### CONCLUSIONS 
### 3. 
### 3.1 
### Factual statements directly connected to the occurrence of 
the accident 
On the basis of available data, the IC concluded that the occurrence was caused 
by a defect of oil cooler of the locomotive. 
On the basis of the findings of site surveys, the IC concluded that a hidden density 
problem developed in the oil cooler of the locomotive, which allowed the oil to leak 
from the closed line and flow onto the smoothing choke coil due to the 
construction of the vehicle. Then the oil caught fire as a result of the rise in the 
temperature of the coil caused by power uptake. 
As a consequence of the fire, the elements of the oil cooler began to leak, and 
large quantity of oil leaked onto the induction coil which was already on fire, thus 
adding more fuel to the fire. 
### 3.2 
### Factual statements indirectly connected to the occurrence 
of the accident 
The operating lever of the stop valve of the transformer oil line is made of a 
material which the heat of the fire was able to melt and destruct, and this made it 
difficult to stop oil leakage during firefighting. 
### 3.3 
### Other risk factors 
The IC has established no such factors.

### 4. 
### SAFETY RECOMMENDATIONS 
### 4.1. 
### Measures taken during the technical investigation 
SOFTRONIC agreed to develop a technical solution for the relocation of the 
smoothing choke coils of the traction motors to a place where they cannot get in 
direct contact with the transformer oil. 
The representatives of SOFTRONIC stated they would analyse also the possibility 
of installing a heat sensor to the smoothing choke coil of each traction motor (6 in 
total) in order to record the data coming from there, and based on this, the main 
breaker of the vehicle can be released if any of the measured values exceeds a 
preset value. 
The representatives of SOFTRONIC stated they would perform the same 
modifications in two other similar locomotives which are in service. 
Following the technical review performed in Craiova, the manufacturer informed its 
business partners, in a letter as well, on the establishments made and on the 
commitments it had taken. 
According to information available to the IC, the locomotive affected in the 
accident was placed in service again 07 March 2014. 
### 4.2. 
### Safety recommendations issued during the technical 
investigation 
No safety recommendation was issued in the course of the technical investigation 
performed by TSB. 
### 4.3. 
### Safety recommendations issued after closing the technical 
investigation 
During the technical review performed at the premises of the manufacturer, the 
manufacturer committed itself to perform modifications to the design of the 
locomotive relevant to the structural factors which had contributed to the 
occurrence of the fire. 
Accordingly, the IC regards it unnecessary to issue safety recommendations.
