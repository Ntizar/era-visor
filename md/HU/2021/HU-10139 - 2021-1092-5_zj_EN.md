### Basic principles of the safety investigation 
The purpose of the safety investigation fulfilled by Transportation Safety Bureau (TSB) as 
National Investigation Body of Hungary is to reveal the causes and circumstances of serious 
railway accidents, railway accidents and railway incidents and propose recommendations in 
order to prevent similar incidents. The safety investigation is not intended to examine and 
determine fault, blame or liability in any form. 
The findings of the safety investigation are based on an assessment of the evidence 
available and obtained by TSB in the course of the investigation, taking into account the 
principles of a fair and impartial procedure. In the Final Report, the persons involved in the 
occurrence shall be referred to by the positions and duties they had at the time of the 
occurrence. 
The Final Report shall not have binding force and no appeal proceedings may be initiated 
against it. 
This safety investigation has been carried out by TSB pursuant to relevant provisions of 
- 
Act CLXXXIV of 2005 on the safety investigation of aviation, railway and marine 
accidents and incidents; 
- 
Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2020/572 of 24 April 2020 on the 
reporting structure to be followed for railway accident and incident investigation 
reports; 
- 
in the absence of other related regulation of the Act CLXXXIV of 2005, the TSB 
conducts the investigation in accordance with Act CL of 2016 on General Public 
Administration Procedures. 
Act CLXXXIV of 2005 is to serve compliance with Directive (EU) 2016/798 of the European 
Parliament and of the Council of 11 May 2016 on railway safety. 
The competence of the TSB is based on Government Regulation № 230/2016. (VII.29.) on 
the assignment of a transportation safety body and on the dissolution of Transportation 
Safety Bureau with legal succession. 
The safety investigation is independent of other investigations, administrative infringement or 
criminal proceedings, as well as proceedings initiated by employers in connection with the 
accident or incident.

### 1. 
### SUMMARY 
On 15 October 2021, at 13:28, three empty wagons (owned by CFR MARFA) of 
the freight train № 56554 from Sajószentpéter to Miskolc-Rendező derailed while 
entering the terminal station. The derailed wagons were regularly transporting salt 
from Romania to Hungary in turnaround trains. 
During the on-site inspection, the root cause of the derailment was found to be the 
brake triangle of the sixth car of the train, which had got detached and stuck in the 
track elements, and this caused two other cars to derail as well. 
The general technical condition of the freight wagon and the other wagons of the 
train performing the same transport task was significantly degraded, with critical 
corrosion of the safety-related parts, and the repair interventions were also carried 
out in an unprofessional manner. 
In the opinion of the IC, the fault that directly caused the derailment could not be 
detected during the car inspections. However, the general technical condition of 
the wagon, which posed a high risk, was known to the forwarding railway 
company, but could not be a reason for the inspector to disallow the wagon to run. 
Basically, the best way to avoid these types of incidents is to maintain your 
wagons properly. If this is outside the direct control of the railway undertaking 
which transports the goods, the railway undertaking can assert its own safety 
interests by carefully choosing its transport partners and by setting and 
consistently maintaining expectations for them. As such measures have been 
taken following the incident, the IC does not consider it justified to issue a safety 
recommendation.

### 4. 
### ANALYSIS OF THE EVENT 
### 4.1 
### Responsibilities of people and organisations 
## 4.1.1 
### Entity in charge of maintenance 
The railway company which operates the rolling stock (CFR MARFA in this case) 
is responsible for ensuring that its rolling stock is in a serviceable condition through 
the entity in charge of maintenance (which for these wagons was itself). The entity 
in charge of maintenance ensures, through its maintenance system, that the 
vehicles it maintains are in a safe operating condition. 
As the course of the accident and the technical condition of the derailing wagon № 
8153 0667 077-0 (4.2) show, the organisation has failed to achieve this objective. 
Indeed, the accident occurred due to a perceptible, recognisable technical 
condition of the wagon, which could have developed over a period of time that 
would have been clearly recognisable at the last workshop inspection (08/09/2021, 
i.e. approximately one month before the accident). 
## 4.1.2 
### The railway company 
At the time of the accident, the car was routed by RCH Zrt. Its task is to ensure 
that the wagon can be safely hauled by carrying out regular inspections of the 
wagon on receipt and during hauling. This task covers visible defects or defects 
that can be measured by simple means. 
As the failure of the structural elements (which can be identified as the direct 
cause of the derailment) occurred during the journey (see: Section 4.2.4), it could 
not be detected during the vehicle inspection. The probable occurrence of such a 
failure in the near future cannot be established by the usual wagon testing 
methods (see 4.1.3 and 4.2.4). 
However, the general technical condition of the car was recognisable and known. 
Both the company and the inspector were aware that the general condition of the 
wagons involved in this transport operation was critical, and that prior to the 
incident, refusal to accept such wagons because of their condition was common. 
As a result, the railway undertaking may take risk mitigation measures in the light 
of the risk: for example, it may modify specifications, impose additional checks for 
the acceptance, or renegotiate relations with the transferring railway undertaking. 
### Such measures were taken following the incident (Hiba! A hivatkozási forrás 
nem található.), but with no detectable effect in the six months following the event, 
no reduction in the number of wagons to be rejected, and a similar event also 
occurred on 1 February 2022 (4.5.2). 
## 4.1.3 
### The wagon inspector 
The wagon inspector is responsible for checking the technical condition of the 
wagons to determine whether they are safe to use in traffic. To this end, he carried 
out an inspection of the outbound train at Sajószentpéter station and a full brake 
test of the train. 
He did not observe any defect that could endanger the safety of the train, as he did 
not prevent the train from starting (accepting that he would not have allowed the 
wagon to run with a recognised defect). On the contrary, the fact of the accident 
and its being attributable to technical reasons prove that there was indeed a 
dangerous defect in the wagon.

### 4.2 
### Rolling stock and technical equipment 
## 4.2.1 
### General condition of the wagon 
The derailment was directly related to the disintegrated brake system of wagon № 
8153 0667 077-0, which, as was also found during the on-site inspection, was 
characterised by the following: 
 the parts were heavily corroded; 
 the pins were loose and damaged; 
 a number of joints were executed in an unprofessional manner. 
A more detailed workshop inspection of the wagon revealed the following 
deficiencies: 
 
The test set-up of the braking equipment (traction gearbox, empty/load 
changeover device, brake disengaging gearbox) and the controls of the 
unloading equipment was attempted by the IC. This was not possible 
without tools and auxiliary equipment, because the large amount of rust 
and corrosion on the pivot and pivot points of the adjusting shafts 
prevented this. Using a chemical rust remover and an auxiliary tool 
(crowbar), it was possible to partially manage these elements. 
 
For further testing, the body was lifted off the bogies on a group hoist. The 
bogies and the body’s coupling elements (turntable and its plastic insert, 
side supports) were worn. These coupling elements had been partially 
replaced during a previous repair prior to the accident, but in a very 
unprofessional manner. The worn parts that were replaced did not conform 
to the size, shape and space tolerances specified. The bogies were 
therefore prevented from running through curves (Figure 4). 
 
The closed bushings of the pin bores of the mechanical brake components 
(brake suspension links, lever pins, brackets) were partially missing. The 
missing bushings were filled by welding, but the holes were not drilled. The 
gaps between the holes and the clevis pins varied between 3 and 5 mm. 
Several of the brake clevises were torn. The surfaces on the drag shoes 
designed to receive the brake blocks were so worn that the brake block 
wedges were unable to hold the brake blocks in position.

Based on the general condition of the wagon, it can be said that a critical level of 
corrosion and the resulting unsafe condition had already existed on the last 
occasion, just over a month before the derailment. Therefore, at the time of the 
workshop brake inspection of the wagon, assuming that it had indeed taken place, 
the poor technical condition was not identified or was identified but there was no 
intent to correct it, i.e. the wagon was not repaired after the workshop inspections 
despite its known defects. 
## 4.2.3 
### The effect of the load 
The wagon, together with about 200 other wagons, regularly transports salt, and 
the customs authorities found residues of it during the on-site inspection. The 
product is characterised by the fact that if it is exposed to moisture - which cannot 
be excluded in a wagon of this construction - the resulting saline water heavily 
corrodes the steel structure of the wagon. 
Corrosion poses many dangers: 
 
the load-bearing, force-transmitting structural elements may break, 
 
the joints can fall apart, 
 
moving parts (e.g. brake assemblies) may become jammed, 
 
etc. 
Examples of these faults were also found by the IC in other cars that derailed and 
were assessed. 
For wagons that regularly transport salt, this is an additional risk due to the load, 
which the companies concerned and the maintenance unit must recognise and 
manage by operating their safety management and maintenance systems. 
However, the massive occurrence of the above deficiencies shows that this is not 
happening, i.e. the risks implied by the cargo have not been managed by the 
maintenance system and the hauling railway companies.

## 4.2.4 
### Detectability of the failure leading to the accident 
In the absence of evidence to the contrary, the IC assumed that the braking 
system had not yet detached when the car was inspected in Sajószentpéter, and 
that the fault had occurred during the journey. The wagon inspector could therefore 
conclude that the wagon was in running order with that brake assembly. It is not 
part of his task, and cannot realistically be done, to examine and judge the 
condition of the anchorages in terms of a probable future detachment. The fact that 
it is visibly corroded does not in itself mean that it will break within a short period of 
time, even a few hundred or a thousand kilometres (or, as in the case of the 
derailment in question, not after 20 km, i.e. only as far as Miskolc); but it certainly 
means that the remaining life of the car is shorter. 
Surfaces corroded to varying degrees are associated with rail vehicles and there is 
no exact limit to the degree of corrosion at which a wagon inspector can cancel a 
wagon on the grounds of corrosion. It is not possible to judge at the level of the 
wagon inspector whether the structures are likely to break or disintegrate in the 
near future.

### 4.4 
### Safety procedures 
### The possibility to detect defective wagons 
The danger of a wagon being in an advanced state of corrosion and the critical 
wear of the fasteners are largely beyond the capability of the inspector to judge or 
check. This would require measurements and detailed inspections that cannot be 
carried out at the level of the wagon inspector. It should therefore be ensured that 
wagons in such a condition are not allowed to enter service in the first place. 
Since this condition (corrosion, wear) develops over a long period of time and 
becomes dangerous over a longer period of time, workshop measurements and 
breakdown tests carried out at appropriate intervals are sufficient to detect 
defective wagons. 
This exists in the railway practice, and if a wagon is in a dangerous condition but it 
is in service nevertheless, 
a) the inspection and maintenance carried out were inadequate because 
defects in the wagon were not detected or corrected; or 
b) the process of degradation is too fast compared to the maintenance cycle. 
Point (a) applies in the present incident, because the wagon underwent a periodic 
inspection of the running gear approximately 1 month before the incident (4.2.2), 
when its dangerous condition was already evident, but was not repaired (4.1.1). 
### Special risks 
Due to the transported material (salt), the corrosion of the wagon structure is 
faster, which raises the problem under (b). However, if the detection and correction 
process does not work, the more frequent inspection cycle will not work either. 
Nevertheless, in addition to correcting the errors in point (a), it may be appropriate 
to carry out a risk analysis of the inspection frequency (Hiba! A hivatkozási forrás 
nem található.).

### 5. 
### CONCLUSIONS 
### 5.1 Summary 
### 5.1.1 Direct causes 
Acts, mistakes, events or conditions or a combination thereof the elimination or 
avoiding of which could probably have prevented the accident or incident: 
a) the wagon’s separated assembly got stuck in the track elements 
and derailed the wagon; 
b) the braking system and its safety clevises were badly corroded and 
fell apart; 
c) the fault was not yet present at the time of the inspection of the 
wagon and its imminent occurrence was not detectable. 
### 5.1.2 Indirect causes 
Acts, mistakes, events or conditions which influenced the occurrence by increasing 
its probability, accelerating the effects or the severity of the consequences, but the 
elimination of which would not have prevented the occurrence: 
a) the condition of the wagon and its known defects were disregarded, 
or repairs were not carried out or were carried out incorrectly. 
### 5.1.3 Systemic factors 
Causal or contributing factors of organisational, management, social or regulatory 
nature which are likely to have an effect on similar or related occurrences, 
particularly including regulatory framework conditions, the design and use of the 
safety management systems, the skills of the personnel, the procedures and 
maintenance: 
a) the maintenance system in place did not ensure good technical 
condition of the wagons; 
b) the railway company maintained the service contract with the 
wagon owner’s partner even in the face of known risks that had 
existed for a long time and in large numbers. 
### 5.2 Actions taken 
Following the incident, RCH carried out more rigorous checks on the wagons, and 
negotiations were opened with the transferor to ensure that the maintenance 
system works properly and that repairs are carried out. 
In the course of the investigation, the IC reviewed the statistics for similar wagons 
for six months after the incident. During that period, the number of rejected wagons 
increased, which may mean that fewer risky wagons are indeed being placed on 
the Hungarian rail network, but it does not prove that the overall condition of the 
wagons has improved. 
The number of rejected wagons increased dramatically in the week when the 
second incident (which did not but not end up in derailment) occurred.

### 5.4 Proven procedures, good practices 
The IC identified no factors that mitigated the consequences of the incident or 
helped to avoid a more serious outcome. 
### 5.5 Lessons learnt 
The best way to avoid these types of incidents is to maintain your wagons 
properly. If this is beyond the direct control of the railway undertaking which 
transports the goods, the railway undertaking can enforce safety interests by 
choosing its freight partners and by consistently maintaining its expectations of 
them. 
Due to the increased corrosion caused by the goods carried, it may be appropriate 
to carry out a risk assessment on the adequacy of the technical inspection 
technology and frequency of the wagons carrying such goods. 
The rail transport authority, acting in its supervisory capacity, may also detect 
wagons in an unsatisfactory technical condition and, if necessary, prohibit their 
participation in traffic or withdraw them from service.

### 6. 
### SAFETY RECOMMENDATION 
Similar incidents can be avoided by proper maintenance of the rolling stock and by 
refusing to take over vehicles in critical condition, and the IC therefore does not 
consider it justified to issue a safety recommendation.
