### Basic principles of the safety investigation 
The purpose of the safety investigation fulfilled by Transportation Safety Bureau (TSB) as 
National Investigation Body of Hungary is to reveal the causes and circumstances of serious 
railway accidents, railway accidents and railway incidents and propose recommendations in 
order to prevent similar incidents. The safety investigation is not intended to examine and 
determine fault, blame or liability in any form. 
The findings of the safety investigation are based on an assessment of the evidence 
available and obtained by TSB in the course of the investigation, taking into account the 
principles of a fair and impartial procedure. In the Final Report, the persons involved in the 
occurrence shall be referred to by the positions and duties they had at the time of the 
occurrence. 
The Final Report shall not have binding force and no appeal proceedings may be initiated 
against it. 
This safety investigation has been carried out by TSB pursuant to relevant provisions of 
- 
Act CLXXXIV of 2005 on the safety investigation of aviation, railway and marine 
accidents and incidents; 
- 
Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2020/572 of 24 April 2020 on the 
reporting structure to be followed for railway accident and incident investigation 
reports; 
- 
in the absence of other related regulation of the Act CLXXXIV of 2005, the TSB 
conducts the investigation in accordance with Act CL of 2016 on General Public 
Administration Procedures. 
Act CLXXXIV of 2005 is to serve compliance with Directive (EU) 2016/798 of the European 
Parliament and of the Council of 11 May 2016 on railway safety. 
The competence of the TSB is based on Government Regulation № 230/2016. (VII.29.) on 
the assignment of a transportation safety body and on the dissolution of Transportation 
Safety Bureau with legal succession. 
The safety investigation is independent of other investigations, administrative infringement or 
criminal proceedings, as well as proceedings initiated by employers in connection with the 
accident or incident.

### 1. 
### SUMMARY 
On 21 October 2022, at 2:57 a.m., the light engine № 14879, leaving Kaposvár 
station, derailed with its front axle in section 297+98, on the plain line. There were 
no personal injuries in the accident. 
The investigation revealed that the locomotive driver of the light engine № 14879, 
leaving the upside-end switching zone at Kaposvár station, was travelling at a 
speed of 40-41 km/h when the right-hand wheel on the front axle of the vehicle’s 
front bogie climbed the right-hand rail and fell over it, and the left-hand wheel fell 
off the left-hand rail in between the rails. The driver, sensing the jolting that 
followed the derailment, applied the brakes quickly, bringing the train to a halt in 
the section 296+85. 
During the investigation following the derailment, it was found that there was a rail 
distortion in the railway track at the accident site that exceeded the out-of-service 
dimension limit, which caused the locomotive to derail. 
Contributing factors to the accident were the inadequate wheel loading of the 
locomotive involved in the accident, which may have increased the risk of 
derailment by impairing the running characteristics, and the existence of a 
substructure defect in the track at the accident site, which had caused a long 
history of repeated failure of track geometry interventions by the local track 
maintenance unit. 
The Investigating Committee identified as a systemic factor that the infrastructure 
operator has no concept for the elimination of the nearly 500 track kilometres of 
substructure defects nationwide, and that substructure repairs are only carried out 
on an ad hoc basis, which does not allow for the long-term elimination of defects at 
the network level. 
The Investigating Committee therefore proposed to issue a safety recommendation 
to establish a strategy to address substructure failures at network level.

### 5. 
### CONCLUSIONS 
### 5.1 
### Summary 
## 5.1.1 
### Direct causes 
Acts, mistakes, events or conditions or a combination thereof the elimination or 
avoiding of which could probably have prevented the accident or incident: 
a) at the site of the accident, there was a plane distortion in the railway track 
that was within the out-of-service limit and caused the locomotive to derail; 
b) the safety defects of the towing vehicle could not be linked to the 
derailment. 
## 5.1.2 
### Indirect causes 
Acts, mistakes, events or conditions which influenced the occurrence by increasing 
its probability, accelerating the effects or the severity of the consequences, but the 
elimination of which would not have prevented the occurrence: 
a) there is a substructure defect in the track where the accident occurred, 
which has caused a long series of ineffective interventions by the local 
track maintenance unit to improve the track geometry. 
## 5.1.3 
### Systemic factors 
Causal or contributing factors of organisational, management, social or regulatory 
nature which are likely to have an effect on similar or related occurrences, 
particularly including regulatory framework conditions, the design and use of the 
safety management systems, the skills of the personnel, the procedures and 
maintenance: 
a) the infrastructure manager has no concept for the repair of the nearly 500 
km of track substructure defects nationwide, and substructure repairs are 
carried out on an ad hoc basis, which does not allow for long-term 
elimination of the problem at network level. 
### 5.2 
### Actions taken 
The IC has not been informed of any measures taken to improve the overall safety 
of rail transport. 
### 5.3 
### Additional notes 
Risk increasing factors that are unrelated to the occurrence of the incident: 
a) the wheels of the locomotive involved in the accident were not properly 
loaded and the locomotive should not have been run. 
### 5.4 
### Proven procedures, good practices 
The IC identified no factor that helped to reduce the consequences of the 
occurence and avoid a more serious outcome. 
### 5.5 
### Lessons learnt

rendered useless by underlying superstructure failures, which cannot be solved by

### 6. 
### SAFETY RECOMMENDATION 
Safety recommendations, together with the findings and conclusions in the final 
investigation report, represent important information for the further improvement of 
railway safety. Accordingly, 
The addressees of safety recommendations can be the rail transport authority 
(ÉKM VHF), other national agencies and authorities, the European Union Railway 
Agency (ERA) or another EU Member State. The organisations responsible for 
implementation (implementers of the safety recommendations) are, within their 
respective areas of responsibility, the contracting railway undertakings, 
infrastructure managers, maintenance organisations and other actors in the railway 
industry. Accordingly: 
-

ensure that safety recommendations are duly taken into consideration and 
applied where appropriate.
