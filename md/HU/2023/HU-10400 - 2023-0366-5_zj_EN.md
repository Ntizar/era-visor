### Basic principles of the safety investigation 
The purpose of the safety investigation fulfilled by Transportation Safety Bureau (TSB) as 
National Investigation Body of Hungary is to reveal the causes and circumstances of serious 
railway accidents, railway accidents and railway incidents and propose recommendations in 
order to prevent similar incidents. The safety investigation is not intended to examine and 
determine fault, blame or liability in any form. 
The findings of the safety investigation are based on an assessment of the evidence 
available and obtained by TSB in the course of the investigation, taking into account the 
principles of a fair and impartial procedure. In the Final Report, the persons involved in the 
occurrence shall be referred to by the positions and duties they had at the time of the 
occurrence. 
The Final Report shall not have binding force and no appeal proceedings may be initiated 
against it. 
This safety investigation has been carried out by TSB pursuant to relevant provisions of 
- 
Act CLXXXIV of 2005 on the safety investigation of aviation, railway and marine 
accidents and incidents; 
- 
Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2020/572 of 24 April 2020 on the 
reporting structure to be followed for railway accident and incident investigation 
reports; 
- 
in the absence of other related regulation of the Act CLXXXIV of 2005, the TSB 
conducts the investigation in accordance with Act CL of 2016 on General Public 
Administration Procedures. 
Act CLXXXIV of 2005 is to serve compliance with Directive (EU) 2016/798 of the European 
Parliament and of the Council of 11 May 2016 on railway safety. 
The competence of the TSB is based on Government Regulation № 230/2016. (VII.29.) on 
the assignment of a transportation safety body and on the dissolution of Transportation 
Safety Bureau with legal succession. 
The safety investigation is independent of other investigations, administrative infringement or 
criminal proceedings, as well as proceedings initiated by employers in connection with the 
accident or incident.

## Página 3

### 1. 
### SUMMARY 
On 15 April 2023, at 5:0 a.m., the freight train No. 42290-1, which was scheduled 
to enter the F24 track of Rákosrendező station, entered the occupied F22 track 
and collided with a stationary train. There were no injuries. 
The accident occurred on a network of tracks where there is no safety installation 
and trains run through unlocked switches. Before the arrival of the train, a shunting 
operation was carried out on the affected tracks of the station, after which the track 
routing was not performed, and no switch control was carried out. 
Station staffing levels have been significantly reduced in recent years, so witch 
control should be carried out by a staff member with a different primary task 
(handling busy traffic). The risk created by the reduction in staff has not been offset 
by the infrastructure manager by the installation of switch locks or other measures. 
The consequences were worsened by the fact that the driver’s attention was not 
sufficiently active. 
As such occurrences can be avoided by following the switch control rules and by 
exercising due care and attention from the staff, the IC did not see any reason to 
propose a safety recommendation.

### 5. 
### CONCLUSIONS 
### 5.1 
### Summary 
## 5.1.1 
### Direct causes 
Acts, mistakes, events or conditions or a combination thereof the elimination or 
avoiding of which could probably have prevented the accident or incident: 
a) the train arrived on the occupied F22 track due to an incorrect 
position of the switches; 
b) the switch check was not carried out. 
## 5.1.2 
### Indirect causes 
Acts, mistakes, events or conditions which influenced the occurrence by increasing 
its probability, accelerating the effects or the severity of the consequences, but the 
elimination of which would not have prevented the occurrence: 
a) the second person is regularly omitted from the switch control; 
b) there is no safety installation the affected track network; 
c) the position of the switches cannot be reliably monitored from the 
train reception point and the possibility of intervention is limited; 
d) the driver's attention was not sufficiently active; 
e) the train was running with slow brakes. 
## 5.1.3 
### Systemic factors 
Causal or contributing factors of organisational, management, social or regulatory 
nature which are likely to have an effect on similar or related occurrences, 
particularly including regulatory framework conditions, the design and use of the 
safety management systems, the skills of the personnel, the procedures and 
maintenance: 
a) the risk created by the reduction in staff over time has not been 
compensated by the infrastructure manager by the installation of 
interchange locks or other measures; 
b) the station technology assigns a second staff member from the staff 
that primarily performs other tasks and handles busy traffic, on a 
case-by-case basis, to perform the switch control. 
### 5.2 
### Actions taken 
In its comments on the draft final report, the railway company indicated that it had 
modified the AVU for Rákosrendező as of 17 July. 
### 5.3 
### Additional notes 
Factors that are not related to the occurrence of the incident but are risk factors: 
a) braking data on the train load statement did not correspond to 
reality; 
b) long-distance trains coming from the end point are moved to the 
appropriate track by a shunting movement instead of being received 
and held on the intended track; 
c) traffic situation and timetable data may contradict each other in 
terms of access speed and direction;

d) for a train arriving on track Z1, the track route after the provided 
access signal may lead to non-closable switches; 
e) the infrastructure manager cannot maintain permanent functioning 
of its audio recording equipment. 
### 5.4 
### Proven procedures, good practices 
As a factor to mitigate the consequences of the incident and avoid a more serious 
outcome, the - in other respects - unfavourable circumstance that the permitted 
and applied speed was low due to the condition of the station’s track network can 
be identified. 
### 5.5 
### Lessons learnt 
Accidents can occur even when a train enters an occupied track, even if at a low 
speed, but performing a switch check can prevent the train from running on a busy 
track due to incorrect switch position. 
The infrastructure manager can compensate for the risk of understaffing by 
providing staff or even by simple technical solutions (switch lock). 
Should an emergency occur nevertheless, proper driver attention would suffice to 
avoid such an accident. 
### 6. 
### SAFETY RECOMMENDATION 
Similar incidents can be avoided by following the rules of switch control and by 
exercising due care and attention from the staff, and therefore the IC does not 
consider it appropriate to issue a safety recommendation.
