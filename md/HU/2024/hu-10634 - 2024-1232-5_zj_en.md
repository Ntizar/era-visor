### Basic principles of the safety investigation 
The purpose of the safety investigation fulfilled by Transportation Safety Bureau (TSB) as 
National Investigation Body of Hungary is to reveal the causes and circumstances of serious 
railway accidents, railway accidents and railway incidents and propose recommendations in 
order to prevent similar incidents. The safety investigation is not intended to examine and 
determine fault, blame or liability in any form. 
The findings of the safety investigation are based on an assessment of the evidence available 
and obtained by TSB in the course of the investigation, taking into account the principles of a 
fair and impartial procedure. In the Final Report, the persons involved in the occurrence shall 
be referred to by the positions and duties they had at the time of the occurrence. 
The Final Report shall not have binding force and no appeal proceedings may be initiated 
against it. 
This safety investigation has been carried out by TSB pursuant to relevant provisions of 
- 
Act CLXXXIV of 2005 on the safety investigation of aviation, railway and marine 
accidents and incidents; 
- 
Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2020/572 of 24 April 2020 on the reporting 
structure to be followed for railway accident and incident investigation reports; 
- 
in the absence of other related regulation of the Act CLXXXIV of 2005, the TSB 
conducts the investigation in accordance with Act CL of 2016 on General Public 
Administration Procedures. 
Act CLXXXIV of 2005 is to serve compliance with Directive (EU) 2016/798 of the European 
Parliament and of the Council of 11 May 2016 on railway safety. 
The competence of the TSB is based on Government Regulation № 230/2016. (VII.29.) on the 
assignment of a transportation safety body and on the dissolution of Transportation Safety 
Bureau with legal succession. 
The safety investigation is independent of other investigations, administrative infringement or 
criminal proceedings, as well as proceedings initiated by employers in connection with the 
accident or incident.

### 1. 
### SUMMARY 
At 10:10 am on 7 November 2024 at Martonvásár station – at the same time as train No. 
4642 was entering the station – train No. 3815, which was standing on Track IV, set off 
without authorisation, passed the individual exit signal (marked ‘K4’) at danger, burst 
points 8 and 6 open, and entered the wrong track (the right-hand track). Realising the 
unexpected situation, the driver began to slow the train down using the service brake, 
then, as the train passed the ‘B’ entry signal behind him, the train control system triggered 
an emergency brake, causing the train to come to a halt 515 metres of the oncoming 
train No. 3542. 
The investigation established that the locomotive driver did not have a firm grasp of how 
to use the ETCS, and therefore selected the NTC-EVM (75 Hz train control) mode, which 
offers a lower level of safety, at the start of the movement. At the scheduled departure 
time, he set the train in motion without authorisation, accelerated to 60 km/h instead of 
the permitted 40 km/h, passed the exit signal marked ‘K4’ at danger, and then burst two 
points open, the incorrect position of which he failed to notice. As the train entered the 
open track, the emergency braking of the 75 Hz train control train control system was 
activated, but the driver had already initiated service braking beforehand. Train No. 3542, 
approaching from the opposite direction, was operating with an ETCS Level 2 train 
control system, and the locomotive driver stopped the train as soon as he noticed that 
there was a train on the same track in the opposite direction. 
The direct cause of the incident can be attributed to a human factor relating to the driver 
of train No. 3815, who, in addition to lacking sufficient technical knowledge, also failed 
to maintain adequate situational awareness. 
The investigation included 
− examination of the human factor; 
− the safety limits of train control equipment; 
− the ETCS training process; 
− the relationship between the safety management system and ETCS. 
The TSB issues a safety recommendation regarding a change to the signal input 
method for train reception tracks equipped for 75 Hz train control.

### 5. 
### CONCLUSIONS 
### 5.1 
### Summary 
## 5.1.1 
### Direct causes 
Acts, mistakes, events or conditions or a combination thereof the elimination or 
avoiding of which could probably have prevented the accident or incident: 
a) The driver of train No. 3815 set off without authorisation and then passed the 
exit signal marked ‘K4’ at danger without permission. 
## 5.1.2 
### Indirect causes 
Acts, mistakes, events or conditions which influenced the occurrence by increasing 
its probability, accelerating the effects or the severity of the consequences, but the 
elimination of which would not have prevented the occurrence: 
a) The driver of train No. 3815 did not select ETCS L2 train control before 
the train departed; 
b) The following factors contributed to the reduced situational awareness of 
the driver of train No. 3815: 
- 
he felt exhausted; 
- 
he felt under time pressure; 
- 
as the departure time approached, the boarding of passengers had a 
distracting effect on him. 
## 5.1.3 
### Systemic factors 
Causal or contributing factors of organisational, management, social or regulatory 
nature which are likely to have an effect on similar or related occurrences, 
particularly including regulatory framework conditions, the design and use of the 
safety management systems, the skills of the personnel, the procedures and 
maintenance: 
a) The training system did not provide the locomotive driver involved in the 
incident with the necessary level of ETCS knowledge to use the equipment 
with confidence; 
b) MÁV Passenger Transport Ltd. has not developed procedures to ensure that 
the train control system is selected in accordance with the regulations. 
### 5.2 
### Actions taken 
The IC is not aware of any measures taken directly in connection with the incident 
that would generally improve railway safety. 
### 5.3 
### Additional notes 
Risk increasing factors that are unrelated to the occurrence of the incident: 
a) The IC did not identify any risk-increasing factors that could be linked to the 
occurrence of the incident. 
### 5.4 
### Proven procedures, good practices

### 5.5 
### Lessons learnt 
The incident served as a lesson showing that 
− in some cases, even a properly functioning train control system does not 
trigger emergency braking; 
− the ETCS Level 2 train control system alone – despite ensuring the highest 
level of safety – does not protect a moving train from all possible unexpected 
events: 
− The ETCS did not detect train No. 3815 entering the authorized route 
of train No. 3542, which was operating at ETCS Level 2, and did not 
revoke train No. 3542’s run authorization. 
− If train No. 3815 had departed at ETCS Level 2, it would not have 
been forced to stop before the signal at danger.

### 6. 
### SAFETY RECOMMENDATION 
Safety recommendations, together with the findings and conclusions in the final 
investigation report, represent important information for the further improvement of 
railway safety. Accordingly, 
The safety recommendations may be addressed to the railway transport authority 
(KBM VHF), other agencies and authorities, the European Union Agency for 
Railways (ERA), and another EU Member State. The organisations responsible for 
introduction (the implementers of the safety recommendations) are, within their 
respective areas of responsibility, the railway undertakings, infrastructure managers, 
organisations responsible for maintenance, and other stakeholders in the railway 
industry. Accordingly: 
- 
The authorities responsible for safety shall take action as necessary to 
ensure that safety recommendations are duly taken into consideration and 
applied where appropriate. 
- 
The organisations responsible for introducing such safety recommendations 
shall start, with no delay, the risk assessment and risk management activities 
related to the contents of such safety recommendation within the procedural 
framework of their safety management system. 
Within 90 days of the issue of the safety recommendation, they shall report back to 
the IC on the actions taken or planned or on their non-acceptance (with justification) 
of such safety recommendation. 
### 6.1 
### BA2024-1232-5-01 
On station tracks equipped for 75 Hz signalling, the signal input comes from one 
direction, from the destination signal corresponding to the train’s direction of travel, 
and does not change until a command is issued from the direction of the other signal. 
Therefore – typically after a locomotive cab changing – the train control system 
operates as if the train were on a section of track not equipped for signalling; the 
role of the automatic protection system must then be taken over by the locomotive 
driver. In this regard, the TSB issues the following safety recommendation: 
Number: 
## BA2024-1232-5-01 
Addressed to: 
### KBM Railway Authority Department 
Responsible for introduction: 
### MÁV Pályaműködtetési Zrt. 
### The TSB recommends the installation of a technical 
### solution on stations receiving tracks equipped for 75 Hz 
train control, whereby signal input is ensured even in the 
event of a locomotive cab changing – from the opposite 
direction of travel – without the need to operate the 
safety equipment. 
If this recommendation is accepted and implemented, the train control system will 
apply emergency braking once the train has passed the exit signal, thereby 
increasing the likelihood that the train will stop before reaching the danger point.
