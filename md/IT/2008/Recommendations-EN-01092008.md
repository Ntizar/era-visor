Recommendations:  
It is necessary to launch and bring to completion a modification process of the standards pertaining to 
the protection of railway work yards on the general assumption that the track switch system will be 
adopted, to be actioned at the same time as the ‘time intervals’.  
If, in certain cases, it is deemed necessary to return to the track release system on signalling, in the 
stated conditions, it is necessary to introduce measures which will increase safety by examining the 
questions linked mainly: 
- to works being carried out in the station (among other things, today there are many stations unmanned 
by a Train Dispatcher, an issue which the IPC should contemplate); 
- to the minimum number of people who need to be solely in charge of the railway yard protection work 
(for example, a sole agent could turn out to be insufficient even in excellent visibility conditions when 
trains are operating in both directions on the tracks); 
- to the maximum number of people who can be employed in protection work (a high number of 
supervisors risks over-complicating the railway yard protection procedure and increases the likelihood of 
human error; beyond a certain number of necessary agents, therefore, the signal protection scheme 
should not be actioned); 
- to deciding on the safety time (for example, regarding discretion in employing the safety space and the 
consequent value of the total minimum safety time); 
-  to the chance to have automatic warning systems and equipment to protect the work yard; 
- to the efficiency of staff training courses; 
- to the possibility of planning work teams’ incident areas and work in order to be able to force trains 
moving on such railway lines to slow down.
